```yaml
scenario:
  id: incident_2016_07_20_homepage_regex_cpu_outage
  title: Malformed post triggers expensive whitespace trim; LB health checks eject all backends

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The site serves user traffic via a load balancer to multiple web frontends, and the LB uses the home page ("/")
    as its health check target. A malformed post displayed on the home page causes the whitespace-trimming routine
    to run extremely slowly and consume CPU on the web frontends. As home page responses exceed the LB health check
    timeout, the LB marks all web servers unhealthy and returns 503 for all endpoints, making the entire site appear down.

  trigger_desc: |
    A post containing an unusually long run of whitespace (tens of thousands of characters) is included in the home page list,
    causing home page rendering to repeatedly execute a very slow whitespace-trimming operation.

  time:
    total_minutes: 40
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 20}
      f: {start_min: 20, end_min: 40}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        User requests for the home page and question pages are routed to any of the four web frontends and complete quickly.
        The load balancer health checks GET / on each backend and keeps all backends in rotation. Monitoring probes report OK.
      flows: [homepage_view_ok, question_view_ok, lb_healthcheck_ok]
      manifestation:
        - load_balancer.lb_pool_status
        - monitoring.mon_probe
        - web_frontend.web_req_completed
        - load_balancer.lb_req_ok

    f:
      desc: |
        Home page rendering becomes CPU-bound due to slow whitespace trimming on a malformed post. Health checks to "/"
        begin timing out and the load balancer progressively removes backends until it has no healthy upstreams for routing,
        at which point it returns 503 for all user requests. During the early slow phase, monitoring may raise a homepage-latency
        alert; once the LB is fast-failing 503s, the dominant monitoring signal becomes the all-backends-unhealthy alert.
        Health checks continue probing the unhealthy backends to detect recovery. Later, an in-progress hotfix rollout restores
        limited capacity, but most requests still fail and the incident remains ongoing by the end of the scenario.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 20
          component: web_frontend
          event: |
            The home page handler starts spending very large CPU time trimming whitespace from a snippet, causing "/" responses
            (including LB health checks) to overrun the proxy timeout and fail.
          why: |
            The trimming uses a backtracking regex-like routine that becomes O(n^2) on long whitespace runs not positioned at end-of-string.
            This is directly observable via slow-trim threshold warnings (elapsed time exceeded) and edge timeouts; additional asynchronous
            abort logs can appear when the app detects client disconnects after timeouts.
          flows: [homepage_slow_timeout, healthcheck_timeout, question_view_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            site_req_no_backend: 0.0
            site_req_ok_degraded: 0.0
            healthcheck_ok_degraded: 0.0
            monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy: 0.0
            monitoring.mon_alert_homepage_latency: 5.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status: 1.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            question_view_ok_f: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: monitoring.mon_incident_update
              count: 1
              hosts: [mon-1]
          manifestation:
            - web_frontend.web_trim_slow
            - load_balancer.lb_healthcheck_fail_timeout
            - load_balancer.lb_req_upstream_timeout
            - monitoring.mon_alert_homepage_latency
            - web_frontend.web_req_aborted
            - load_balancer.lb_pool_status

        - order: 2
          at_min: 24
          component: load_balancer
          event: |
            After repeated timeouts on the "/" health check, the load balancer marks all web frontends unhealthy for routing
            and begins returning 503 for user traffic because no healthy upstreams remain.
          why: |
            The LB's routing pool membership is directly driven by health check success; using "/" as the health check couples a home-page-only
            regression to full site availability at the edge. When the LB fast-fails 503 due to no healthy upstreams, end-user latency typically
            drops while availability collapses; the homepage-latency alert is therefore suppressed and the all-backends-unhealthy alert becomes primary.
          flows: [site_req_no_backend, healthcheck_timeout]
          rate_multipliers:
            homepage_slow_timeout: 0.0
            question_view_ok_f: 0.0
            healthcheck_timeout: 1.0
            healthcheck_ok_degraded: 0.0
            site_req_no_backend: 1.0
            site_req_ok_degraded: 0.0
            monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy: 1.0
            monitoring.mon_alert_homepage_latency: 0.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status: 0.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy: 1.0
            web_frontend.web_req_aborted: 0.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: load_balancer.lb_backend_marked_unhealthy
              count: 4
              hosts: [lb-1]
            - ref: monitoring.mon_incident_update
              count: 1
              hosts: [mon-1]
          manifestation:
            - load_balancer.lb_backend_marked_unhealthy
            - load_balancer.lb_req_no_backend
            - load_balancer.lb_healthcheck_fail_timeout
            - load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy
            - monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy

        - order: 3
          at_min: 34
          component: web_frontend
          event: |
            A hotfix rollout starts on part of the web pool, and the LB begins seeing some backends pass health checks again.
            Some requests succeed, but the majority still receive 503 due to insufficient healthy capacity and pool instability.
          why: |
            The code change is an operational action (deploy marker). As some backends start responding to GET / within the health check timeout,
            the LB marks at least one backend healthy again; however, most user traffic still fails fast at the edge due to limited healthy capacity.
          flows: [site_req_ok_degraded, site_req_no_backend, healthcheck_ok_degraded, healthcheck_timeout]
          rate_multipliers:
            site_req_no_backend: 0.8
            site_req_ok_degraded: 0.2
            healthcheck_timeout: 0.5
            healthcheck_ok_degraded: 0.5
            monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy: 0.5
            monitoring.mon_alert_homepage_latency: 0.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status: 1.0
            load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy: 0.3
            web_frontend.web_req_aborted: 0.3
          latency_multipliers:
            site_req_ok_degraded: {p50: 1.8, p95: 2.5}
            healthcheck_ok_degraded: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: web_frontend.web_deploy
              count: 1
              hosts: [web-2]
            - ref: load_balancer.lb_backend_marked_healthy
              count: 1
              hosts: [lb-1]
          manifestation:
            - web_frontend.web_deploy
            - load_balancer.lb_backend_marked_healthy
            - load_balancer.lb_req_no_backend
            - load_balancer.lb_req_ok
            - load_balancer.lb_healthcheck_ok
            - load_balancer.lb_pool_status

      steady:
        - component: load_balancer
          condition: |
            The LB remains in a degraded state where most requests fail fast due to no healthy upstreams for routing, with intermittent brief periods
            where one backend passes health checks and some requests succeed.
          user_impact: |
            Users see widespread 503 errors and an effectively unavailable site; a minority of requests may intermittently load.
          manifestation:
            - load_balancer.lb_req_no_backend
            - load_balancer.lb_req_ok
            - load_balancer.lb_pool_status
            - load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy
            - monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy

        - component: web_frontend
          condition: |
            Some web nodes remain under abnormal CPU/queue pressure from the expensive whitespace trimming behavior; when health checks hit an affected node,
            slow-trim warnings can occur. On nodes where the hotfix is deployed, requests are more likely to complete.
          user_impact: |
            If routed to a frontend, the home page may be extremely slow or fail; otherwise the edge returns 503 before reaching the app.
          manifestation:
            - web_frontend.web_cpu_metric
            - web_frontend.web_trim_slow
            - web_frontend.web_req_aborted

      feedback_loops:
        - id: homepage_healthcheck_coupling
          desc: |
            The LB uses GET / as the health check; when "/" becomes slow due to CPU-bound rendering, health checks fail and remove backends.
            Once backends are removed for routing, all endpoints fail at the edge even if non-homepage endpoints would otherwise work.

      flows:
        - homepage_slow_timeout
        - healthcheck_timeout
        - site_req_no_backend
        - site_req_ok_degraded
        - healthcheck_ok_degraded
      manifestation:
        - load_balancer.lb_req_upstream_timeout
        - load_balancer.lb_req_no_backend
        - load_balancer.lb_pool_status
        - load_balancer.lb_pool_status_all_unhealthy
        - load_balancer.lb_healthcheck_fail_timeout
        - load_balancer.lb_healthcheck_ok
        - web_frontend.web_trim_slow
        - web_frontend.web_req_aborted
        - monitoring.mon_alert_homepage_latency
        - monitoring.mon_alert_all_backends_unhealthy

  assumptions:
    - |
        The real incident lasted ~34 minutes; this scenario is kept at 40 minutes total (20 normal + 20 failure) so the failure begins near mid-scenario,
        and it ends while still degraded (partial recovery) to satisfy the "scenario ends in failure" requirement.
    - |
        Failure progression is simplified into three beats: (1) CPU-bound "/" rendering causing edge timeouts, (2) LB ejects all backends for routing and serves 503,
        (3) partial rollout yields intermittent success but continued major outage.
    - |
        Successful modeled endpoints always return HTTP 200 (no 302 redirects) to keep web_frontend.web_req_completed and load_balancer.lb_req_ok semantically consistent.
    - |
        web_frontend.web_trim_slow is treated as a threshold warning (elapsed time exceeded) emitted before request completion; its elapsed_ms domain is kept below the LB timeout range
        so timeout flow log order (app warning then LB timeout) remains chronologically plausible.
    - |
        The deploy one-shot is intended to represent the start of the hotfix rollout; the system model constrains web_deploy.stage to 'start' to prevent contradictory emissions.
    - |
        Homepage-latency alerting is modeled as present primarily during the initial slow-timeout phase and suppressed once the LB is fast-failing 503s, aligning alerting with symptom change.
    - |
        Piecewise volume estimate (order-of-magnitude):
        - Normal (0–20, 20 min): ~1488 flow-logs/min + ~7 background/min ≈ ~29.9k logs
        - Failure seg1 (20–24, 4 min): ~1248 flow-logs/min + ~16 background/min ≈ ~5.1k logs
        - Failure seg2 (24–34, 10 min): ~768 flow-logs/min + ~7.2 background/min ≈ ~7.8k logs
        - Failure seg3 (34–40, 6 min): ~912 flow-logs/min + ~10.1 background/min ≈ ~5.5k logs
        Total ≈ ~48.2k logs (+ ~8 one-shots), within 20k–100k.
    - |
        One-shot counts per event: event1=1 incident_update; event2=4 backend_marked_unhealthy + 1 incident_update; event3=1 deploy + 1 backend_marked_healthy.
    - |
        Visibility rationale: slow-trim warnings (web_trim_slow) plus LB healthcheck timeout logs are frequent early in failure; a brief burst of homepage-latency alerts is likely due to
        the elevated multiplier during 20–24. The transition to total ejection is corroborated by backend_marked_unhealthy, pool_status_all_unhealthy, and the 503 surge; partial recovery
        is visible as a mix of 503s and occasional 200s plus health checks that sometimes succeed (lb_healthcheck_ok) and a backend_marked_healthy marker. Pool snapshots are constrained
        to be physically coherent with 4 total backends. Monitoring probes are allowed to be either slow (timeout-like) or fast (immediate 503) depending on LB routing state.
```
