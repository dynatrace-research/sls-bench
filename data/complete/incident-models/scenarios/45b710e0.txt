scenario:
  id: arpanet_1980_routing_update_cycle
  title: Routing update sequence-number cycle causes network-wide IMP resource starvation

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    IMP nodes normally flood routing updates and TIP users can open remote sessions through stable neighbor lines.
    A hardware fault leads to malformed/corrupted routing update sequence numbers for updates originating at IMP50,
    creating a cycle (8/40/44) that is repeatedly accepted/forwarded and overwhelms IMP CPU and buffers.
    As line protocol messages are starved, many neighbor lines are declared down and TIP users see widespread "net trouble" errors.

  trigger_desc: |
    A flaky neighbor link and memory/table corruption during routing update retransmission causes routing updates
    that claim origin=IMP50 to circulate with the mutually "later" sequence numbers 8, 40, and 44.

    total_minutes: 30
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 15}
      f: {start_min: 15, end_min: 30}

      desc: |
        TIP users routinely open sessions via their local IMP. IMPs flood routing updates at a bounded rate,
        CPU is mostly available, buffers are healthy, and neighbor lines are generally stable.
      flows: [tip_connect_success, routing_update_processed_n]
      manifestation: [imp_node.cpu_stat, imp_node.buf_stat, imp_node.ru_rx_normal, tip_access.conn_ok]

    f:
      desc: |
        A repeating set of routing updates claiming origin IMP50 (seq 8/40/44) begins circulating broadly.
        IMP routing processing consumes CPU and buffers, causing update backlogs and buffer starvation.
        As starvation persists, line up/down protocol processing fails (hello timeouts), lines flap down, and most TIP sessions fail.
