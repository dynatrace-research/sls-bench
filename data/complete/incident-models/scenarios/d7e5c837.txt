scenario:
  id: chrome_sync_throttle_unknown_type_crash
  title: Chrome SyncThread crashes during quota-service incident (unknown throttled datatype)

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    Chrome clients periodically run sync cycles against a Sync Server that depends on a Quota Service to
    decide per-datatype throttling. A faulty load-balancer configuration change overloads the Quota Service,
    and Sync Servers react by telling clients to throttle all datatypes. Some responses include throttled
    datatype identifiers unknown to older/stable clients; due to a client-side enum-set/parsing bug, the
    SyncThread throws std::out_of_range and Chrome crashes. Users experience repeated browser crashes and
    missing crash reports due to intermittent crash upload failures.

  trigger_desc: |
    A load-balancer configuration rollout for traffic routed to the Quota Service is applied without an
    explanatory comment and unintentionally skews/overconcentrates traffic onto a subset of quota-service
    backends. This change happens in shared infrastructure and precedes any visible Chrome Sync symptoms;
    the first internal effect is quota-service queue growth and latency increases observed by callers.

  time:
    total_minutes: 30
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 15}
      f: {start_min: 15, end_min: 30}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Chrome clients are steadily running scheduled sync jobs. Each sync cycle posts a protobuf request to
        the Sync Server (sync_cycle_success), which performs a quota check and writes sync metadata before
        returning a 200 response. Clients typically see an OK status and an empty throttled_types list.
        Operationally, backend services show stable health logs and there are no widespread error patterns.
        Occasional unrelated crash uploads succeed, but they are infrequent and not clustered.
      flows: [sync_cycle_success, crash_report_upload_success]
      manifestation:
        - chrome_client.sync_job_start
        - sync_server.sync_req_received
        - sync_server.sync_resp_200
        - chrome_client.sync_response

    f:
      desc: |
        The incident begins with an infrastructure load-balancer change that degrades the Quota Service.
        Initially, Sync Servers still succeed for many requests but observe rising quota RPC tail latency.
        As the Quota Service becomes unreliable, Sync Servers begin responding conservatively by instructing
        clients to throttle all datatypes, and some requests fail with 503s that trigger client retries.
        A subset of throttle-all responses contains throttled datatype identifiers not recognized by older
        clients; these clients hit an unhandled std::out_of_range in the sync thread and crash repeatedly.
        The increased crash volume and shared infrastructure strain also cause intermittent crash upload
        failures, leading to missing crash entries on some clients.
      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 15
          component: traffic_lb
          event: |
            A new LB configuration is applied for quota-service routing, and backend pool imbalance warnings
            begin appearing while quota-service telemetry shows rising queue depth.
          why: |
            Skewed routing concentrates traffic on a subset of quota-service backends, increasing queueing
            delay and tail latency. This is directly observable from traffic_lb.pool_imbalance and
            quota_service.overload_warn; sync servers then surface the dependency strain via
            sync_server.quota_rpc_slow. The exact misconfiguration details (rule/weights) require config
            plane inspection outside the logs.
          flows: [sync_cycle_ok_degraded, sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry]
          rate_multipliers:
            sync_cycle_throttle_known: 0.0
            sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash: 0.0
            crash_report_upload_fail_retry: 0.0
            sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry: 0.2
            quota_service.overload_warn: 1.5
            sync_server.quota_rpc_slow: 1.2
          one_shots:
            - ref: traffic_lb.config_change_applied
              count: 1
              hosts: [lb_01]
            - ref: traffic_lb.config_change_no_comment
              count: 1
              hosts: [lb_01]
          manifestation:
            - traffic_lb.config_change_applied
            - traffic_lb.pool_imbalance
            - quota_service.overload_warn

        - order: 2
          at_min: 18
          component: sync_server
          event: |
            Sync Servers start replying to a growing fraction of /sync requests with throttle-all responses,
            while some requests fail with 503 and clients begin scheduling retries.
          why: |
            With quota decisions unavailable or too slow, the Sync Server enters a safety/fail-closed path:
            instead of risking quota overruns, it instructs clients to throttle broadly and sometimes returns
            503 when dependency timeouts are exceeded. This transition is directly observable via an increase
            in sync_server.sync_resp_throttle_all and sync_server.sync_resp_503, plus client-side retry logs
            chrome_client.sync_retry_scheduled in sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry.
          flows: [sync_cycle_throttle_known, sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry]
          rate_multipliers:
            sync_cycle_ok_degraded: 0.8
            sync_cycle_throttle_known: 0.8
            sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry: 1.0
            crash_report_upload_fail_retry: 0.2
            sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash: 0.0
            quota_service.overload_warn: 2.0
            sync_server.quota_rpc_slow: 1.5
          latency_multipliers:
            sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.6}
            sync_cycle_throttle_known: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: sync_server.fail_closed_enabled
              count: 1
              hosts: [sync_01]
          manifestation:
            - sync_server.sync_resp_throttle_all
            - sync_server.sync_resp_503
            - chrome_client.sync_retry_scheduled

        - order: 3
          at_min: 20
          component: chrome_client
          event: |
            A subset of clients begins crashing on the Chrome_SyncThread immediately after receiving
            throttle-all responses; these clients log unknown specifics field numbers during parsing.
          why: |
            Throttle-all responses sometimes include unrecognized datatype identifiers (modeled as an unknown
            specifics field number). A client-side bug converts the unknown identifier to an UNSPECIFIED enum
            value and then uses it in an EnumSet/bitset indexed from FIRST_REAL_MODEL_TYPE, producing a
            negative offset that triggers std::out_of_range and abort. The presence of unknown inputs is
            directly observable from chrome_client.throttle_parse_warning and the immediate crash sequence
            (chrome_client.sync_exception -> chrome_client.chrome_crash) in sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash;
            the precise offset/EnumSet mechanism requires code knowledge beyond logs.
          flows: [sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash, sync_cycle_throttle_known]
          rate_multipliers:
            sync_cycle_ok_degraded: 0.6
            sync_cycle_throttle_known: 1.0
            sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash: 1.2
            sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry: 1.2
            crash_report_upload_fail_retry: 1.0
            quota_service.overload_warn: 2.5
            sync_server.quota_rpc_slow: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash: {p50: 1.1, p95: 1.2}
            sync_cycle_ok_degraded: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.6}
          manifestation:
            - chrome_client.throttle_parse_warning
            - chrome_client.sync_exception
            - chrome_client.chrome_crash

        - order: 4
          at_min: 23
          component: crash_collector
          event: |
            Crash upload failures increase sharply with 429/503 responses, and clients begin dropping some
            crash reports after retries, while sync traffic remains dominated by throttling and retries.
          why: |
            Crash volume rises due to repeated client crashes and restarts, and shared infrastructure strain
            reduces crash collector capacity, resulting in 429/503 responses. This is directly observable from
            crash_collector.upload_failed and the elevated crash upload retry flow (crash_report_upload_fail_retry).
            The coupling to shared infrastructure is only partially observable from these logs and generally
            requires external infrastructure telemetry to confirm.
          flows: [crash_report_upload_fail_retry, sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry, sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash]
          rate_multipliers:
            crash_report_upload_fail_retry: 1.3
            sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash: 1.5
            sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry: 1.3
            sync_cycle_ok_degraded: 0.5
            quota_service.overload_warn: 3.0
            sync_server.quota_rpc_slow: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            crash_report_upload_fail_retry: {p50: 1.4, p95: 2.0}
          manifestation:
            - crash_collector.upload_failed
            - chrome_client.crash_upload_done
            - sync_server.sync_resp_throttle_all

      steady:
        - component: quota_service
          condition: |
            Quota Service remains overloaded with sustained queueing and elevated tail latency, producing
            frequent overload warnings and missed deadlines for callers.
          user_impact: |
            Indirect: Chrome Sync becomes unstable and/or heavily throttled; retries increase and overall
            sync freshness degrades.
          manifestation: [quota_service.overload_warn, quota_service.health_ok]

        - component: sync_server
          condition: |
            Sync Servers operate in a conservative fail-closed posture: many requests are answered with
            throttle-all instructions and some with 503 after quota dependency timeouts.
          user_impact: |
            Users see delayed sync, features that depend on sync stall, and some clients enter retry loops.
          manifestation: [sync_server.quota_rpc_slow, sync_server.sync_resp_throttle_all, sync_server.sync_resp_503]

        - component: chrome_client
          condition: |
            A subset of clients crashes on the SyncThread when parsing throttle-all instructions containing
            unrecognized datatype identifiers; others survive but remain throttled or retrying.
          user_impact: |
            Repeated Chrome crashes (especially around sync activity); sync settings may appear unreliable
            and the browser may restart/crash-loop.
          manifestation: [chrome_client.throttle_parse_warning, chrome_client.chrome_crash, chrome_client.sync_retry_scheduled]

        - component: crash_collector
          condition: |
            Crash Collector intermittently rejects uploads due to bursty crash volume and reduced capacity,
            causing some crash reports not to appear in client crash lists.
          user_impact: |
            Users/engineers may not see crashes recorded (missing crash entries) even when Chrome visibly crashes.
          manifestation: [crash_collector.upload_failed, chrome_client.crash_upload_done]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_and_crash_amplification
          desc: |
            Quota-service overload increases quota RPC timeouts -> Sync Server returns 503/throttle-all ->
            clients retry more frequently and some crash -> restarts and retries create additional sync traffic ->
            additional load on shared infrastructure and quota-service -> even more timeouts and throttle-all.

      flows:
        - sync_cycle_ok_degraded
        - sync_cycle_throttle_known
        - sync_cycle_throttle_unknown_crash
        - sync_cycle_quota_timeout_retry
        - crash_report_upload_fail_retry

      manifestation:
        - traffic_lb.pool_imbalance
        - quota_service.overload_warn
        - sync_server.quota_rpc_slow
        - sync_server.sync_resp_throttle_all
        - sync_server.sync_resp_503
        - chrome_client.throttle_parse_warning
        - chrome_client.sync_exception
        - chrome_client.chrome_crash
        - chrome_client.sync_retry_scheduled
        - crash_collector.upload_failed
        - chrome_client.crash_upload_done

  assumptions:
    - >
      The post-mortem mentions a broad Google outage (initially framed as "Gmail is down") and a later root-cause
      clarification. This scenario focuses on the confirmed mechanism: quota-service degradation due to a faulty
      load-balancer config and the client crash due to unrecognized throttled datatypes.
    - >
      The timeline is compressed into a 30-minute window with failure starting mid-scenario; progression is modeled
      in four events (config change -> server fail-closed throttling -> client parsing crash -> crash upload failures)
      to reflect onset and escalation without introducing a recovery phase.
    - >
      Log-volume estimate (consistent with the system description’s rates):
      Normal phase ~1,148 logs/min * 15 min ~= ~17,220.
      Failure phase piecewise ~= ~37,540 (including ~2 one-shots).
      Total ~= ~54,760 lines; failure is noisier due to retries and crash loops, matching the incident character.
    - >
      The “unknown throttled datatype” is represented both as a human-readable UNKNOWN_TYPE in throttled_types and as
      a numeric specifics field number in chrome_client.throttle_parse_warning; the numeric mapping is an abstraction
      of protobuf field-number-to-enum conversion described in the post-mortem.
