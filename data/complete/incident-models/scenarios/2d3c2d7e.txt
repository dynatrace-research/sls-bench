scenario:
  id: cf_route_leak_to_offline_hkg
  title: BGP route leak attracts global Anycast traffic to offline Hong Kong POP

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The system is an Anycast CDN edge where most traffic arrives via a trusted transit provider and a smaller portion via direct peers.
    During scheduled Hong Kong maintenance, the HK site is effectively offline for inbound service, but a misapplied routing policy causes the HK router to announce CDN-wide prefixes.
    The transit provider propagates the update, pulling most global transit traffic toward Hong Kong and causing widespread HTTP timeouts, while direct peers continue to route to healthy POPs.
    Operators withdraw the bad routes and, within about 15 minutes of the initial leak, routing and probe success rates return to normal levels.

  trigger_desc: |
    During Hong Kong maintenance, an operator accidentally applies an outbound export policy on an inbound-facing interface, causing the HK router to originate the full CDN prefix set via BGP while the site is not serving inbound traffic.

  time:
    total_minutes: 31
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 16}
      f: {start_min: 16, end_min: 31}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Transit and direct peers route user HTTP traffic to healthy POPs (Singapore/Tokyo). Synthetic probes succeed with low latency.
        Periodic edge stats show stable request volume and connection counts, and peering stats show steady peer-delivered traffic.
      flows: [user_http_transit_n, user_http_peer_n, synthetic_probe_n]
      manifestation:
        - edge_pop_other.access_transit
        - edge_pop_other.access_peer
        - route_monitor.probe_ok
        - route_monitor.rib_summary
        - edge_pop_other.edge_stats
        - direct_peer.peer_stats

    f:
      desc: |
        The HK router begins advertising CDN-wide prefixes to the transit provider. Because the provider trusts the announcement, routes propagate and many
        networks select the HK path, sending traffic to a POP that is effectively offline. Served transit traffic at non-HK POPs collapses and synthetic probes
        increasingly time out. After operators withdraw the announcement, probe timeouts drop and transit-served traffic returns as BGP reconverges, completing
        recovery within the incident window.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 16
          component: hkg_router
          event: |
            A misapplied policy causes the Hong Kong router to announce a large set of CDN prefixes to the trusted transit provider, and transit-delivered traffic to healthy POPs begins dropping.
          why: |
            Outbound export routes were entered on an inbound-facing interface, effectively leaking the full CDN prefix set from HK. This is directly observable through BGP update markers
            and indirectly through reduced transit-served access volume and increased synthetic probe timeouts.
          flows: [user_http_transit_f, user_http_peer_f, synthetic_probe_ok_f, synthetic_probe_timeout_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            user_http_transit_f: 0.2
            synthetic_probe_ok_f: 0.8
            synthetic_probe_timeout_f: 1.2
            route_monitor.route_leak_alert: 5.0
            route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert: 2.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: netcfg_tool.change_applied
              count: 1
              hosts: [netcfg-1]
            - ref: hkg_router.bgp_announce
              count: 1
              hosts: [rtr-hkg-1]
            - ref: upstream_transit.bgp_update_received
              count: 1
              hosts: [transit-1]
          manifestation:
            - hkg_router.bgp_announce
            - upstream_transit.bgp_update_received
            - route_monitor.route_leak_alert
            - route_monitor.probe_timeout

        - order: 2
          at_min: 20
          component: route_monitor
          event: |
            As the leaked routes propagate further, transit-delivered served traffic at non-HK POPs falls to near-zero and probe timeouts dominate.
          why: |
            BGP propagation causes more networks to prefer the HK-originated path, blackholing a larger fraction of transit traffic. This is observable as a sharp
            drop in transit access logs at healthy POPs and a sustained increase in synthetic probe timeouts; leak and traffic-drop alerts fire more frequently.
          flows: [user_http_transit_f, synthetic_probe_ok_f, synthetic_probe_timeout_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            user_http_transit_f: 0.02
            synthetic_probe_ok_f: 0.2
            synthetic_probe_timeout_f: 1.8
            route_monitor.route_leak_alert: 8.0
            route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert: 6.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: upstream_transit.bgp_propagation_notice
              count: 1
              hosts: [transit-1]
          manifestation:
            - route_monitor.probe_timeout
            - route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert
            - route_monitor.route_leak_alert

        - order: 3
          at_min: 27
          component: netcfg_tool
          event: |
            Operators push the corrected policy and withdraw the bad HK announcements; probe successes become common again and transit-served traffic begins returning.
          why: |
            Withdrawing the leaked prefixes removes the HK-attracting path from the transit provider, but Internet-wide routing still needs time to reconverge.
            This is observable as fewer probe timeouts and a reduction in leak/traffic-drop alerts while transit access volume rises from near-zero.
          flows: [user_http_transit_f, synthetic_probe_ok_f, synthetic_probe_timeout_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            user_http_transit_f: 0.4
            synthetic_probe_ok_f: 1.8
            synthetic_probe_timeout_f: 0.2
            route_monitor.route_leak_alert: 2.0
            route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert: 3.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: netcfg_tool.change_reverted
              count: 1
              hosts: [netcfg-1]
            - ref: hkg_router.bgp_withdraw
              count: 1
              hosts: [rtr-hkg-1]
            - ref: upstream_transit.bgp_update_received
              count: 1
              hosts: [transit-1]
          manifestation:
            - hkg_router.bgp_withdraw
            - netcfg_tool.change_reverted
            - route_monitor.probe_ok
            - route_monitor.probe_timeout

        - order: 4
          at_min: 30
          component: upstream_transit
          event: |
            Route selection across the transit path stabilizes after withdrawal propagation; probe timeouts stop and transit-delivered traffic returns to normal levels.
          why: |
            As BGP reconverges after the withdrawal, the leaked HK origin is no longer selected and traffic resumes reaching healthy POPs. This is observable as
            the disappearance of probe timeouts and route-leak/traffic-drop alerts, alongside restored transit-served access volume.
          flows: [user_http_transit_f, synthetic_probe_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            user_http_transit_f: 1.0
            synthetic_probe_ok_f: 2.0
            synthetic_probe_timeout_f: 0.0
            route_monitor.route_leak_alert: 0.0
            route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert: 0.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation:
            - route_monitor.probe_ok
            - edge_pop_other.access_transit

      steady:
        - component: upstream_transit
          condition: |
            Transit routing has reconverged after the HK withdrawal; the leaked route is no longer attracting traffic.
          user_impact: |
            Transit-delivered users regain normal reachability; widespread timeouts cease.
          manifestation:
            - route_monitor.probe_ok
            - edge_pop_other.access_transit
        - component: direct_peer
          condition: |
            Direct peering remains stable throughout and continues delivering its share of traffic to healthy POPs.
          user_impact: |
            Users behind direct peers see little or no impact during the incident window.
          manifestation:
            - direct_peer.peer_stats
            - edge_pop_other.access_peer
        - component: edge_pop_other
          condition: |
            Healthy POPs return to serving both transit and peer traffic at typical levels.
          user_impact: |
            End-user performance and availability return to baseline.
          manifestation:
            - edge_pop_other.edge_stats
            - edge_pop_other.access_transit
            - edge_pop_other.access_peer

      feedback_loops:
        - id: bgp_trust_propagation_loop
          desc: |
            The transit provider trusts customer announcements and rapidly propagates BGP updates to its upstreams; this amplifies the blast radius
            because more networks select the leaked HK path, further reducing the fraction of traffic that reaches healthy POPs until withdrawal reconverges.

      flows: [user_http_transit_f, user_http_peer_f, synthetic_probe_ok_f, synthetic_probe_timeout_f]
      manifestation:
        - route_monitor.route_leak_alert
        - route_monitor.edge_traffic_drop_alert
        - route_monitor.probe_timeout
        - edge_pop_other.edge_stats
        - direct_peer.peer_stats
        - edge_pop_other.access_peer
        - edge_pop_other.access_transit

  assumptions:
    - "Failure duration is modeled as 15 minutes (16..31), matching the post-mortem's maximum downtime and ~15-minute network-wide correction window."
    - "Piecewise failure breakdown (minutes): 16-20 leak begins; 20-27 near-total transit blackhole; 27-30 recovery in progress after withdrawal; 30-31 stabilized recovery."
    - "Effective request rates during failure (approx): user_http_peer_f stays ~300 rpm; user_http_transit_f ~180 rpm (0.2x) then ~18 rpm (0.02x) then ~360 rpm (0.4x) then ~900 rpm (1.0x)."
    - "Probes remain ~60 rpm total in failure via ok/timeout split: 16-20 (ok~24 rpm, timeout~36 rpm), 20-27 (ok~6 rpm, timeout~54 rpm), 27-30 (ok~54 rpm, timeout~6 rpm), 30-31 (ok~60 rpm, timeout~0)."
    - "One-shot counts: event1=3 markers (config applied, BGP announce, transit received), event2=1 marker (propagation notice), event3=3 markers (revert, withdraw, transit received), event4=0."
    - "Total log estimate: normal ~20.2k + failure ~8.3k ≈ 28.5k logs. Failure signals are likely visible because probe_timeout is frequent during 20-27, route_leak_alert repeats during the leak, and access_transit volume returns sharply once corrected."
