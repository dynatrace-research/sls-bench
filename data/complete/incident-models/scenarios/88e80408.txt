```yaml
scenario:
  id: us_region_service_discovery_thundering_herd_2020_09_24
  title: Service discovery quorum loss from uncached NXDOMAIN DNS storm

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The system serves an interactive web UI/API and ingests telemetry, with most components depending on internal
    service discovery and dynamic configuration via node-local DNS resolvers. A routine recycle of a latency-tracker
    cluster removes its discovery record, producing uncached NXDOMAIN answers that trigger rapid repeated DNS lookups.
    The resulting thundering herd destabilizes the service-discovery cluster, leading to intermittent dependency/config
    resolution failures and high 5xx rates from the web tier, while telemetry ingestion continues to accept data.

  trigger_desc: |
    The latency-tracker cluster is recycled (scaled down and back up), temporarily removing its service-discovery entry.
    Because NXDOMAIN answers are not cached by the node-local resolvers, clients repeatedly query for the missing record,
    rapidly increasing DNS traffic to the service-discovery cluster.

  time:
    total_minutes: 50
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 20}
      f: {start_min: 20, end_min: 50}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Web requests usually succeed with only rare transient 503s. Telemetry payloads are accepted at intake.
        The intake-side latency tracking lookup is typically served from the node-local DNS cache (positive caching),
        resulting in low upstream DNS load on service discovery. Service discovery emits stable raft health snapshots.
      flows: [ui_widget_ok, ui_widget_backend_503, ui_widget_lookup_failed, payload_ingest, latency_tracker_dns_ok]
      manifestation:
        - web_frontend.access_ok
        - backend_api.req_ok
        - intake_cluster.payload_accepted
        - dns_resolver.cache_hit
        - service_discovery.raft_state

    f:
      desc: |
        After the latency-tracker record disappears, resolvers repeatedly forward NXDOMAIN responses upstream because they
        do not cache NXDOMAIN. The spike in DNS load pushes the service-discovery cluster into instability: quorum is
        intermittently lost and DNS answers degrade into SERVFAIL and timeouts. Backend services struggle to refresh
        runtime configuration, and the web tier sees high rates of lookup failures and downstream 503s. Data ingestion
        remains successful, but latency tracking is often skipped.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 20
          component: latency_tracker
          event: |
            Latency-tracker cluster recycling starts; its discovery entry is missing long enough for clients to receive
            NXDOMAIN and repeatedly re-query, sharply increasing forwarded DNS lookups.
          why: |
            NXDOMAIN responses are intentionally not cached by the node-local resolver, so a missing record causes rapid
            repeat queries (directly observable via cache_miss_nxdomain and upstream NXDOMAIN answers). The resulting load
            begins stressing the consensus-based discovery cluster, which is partially observable via quorum_lost.
          flows: [latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain, payload_ingest, ui_widget_ok, ui_widget_backend_503, ui_widget_lookup_failed]
          rate_multipliers:
            latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain: 1.5
            latency_tracker_dns_servfail: 0.0
            latency_tracker_dns_timeout: 0.0
            service_discovery.quorum_lost: 2.0
            backend_api.config_refresh_failed: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            ui_widget_lookup_failed: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: latency_tracker.cluster_recycle
              count: 1
              hosts: [lt-1]
          manifestation:
            - dns_resolver.cache_miss_nxdomain
            - service_discovery.dns_nxdomain
            - service_discovery.quorum_lost

        - order: 2
          at_min: 30
          component: service_discovery
          event: |
            After caches expire across more nodes, the DNS thundering herd expands and service discovery becomes more
            unstable, producing SERVFAIL and timeouts alongside continued NXDOMAIN traffic.
          why: |
            Once client-side caches and resolver caches refresh, more nodes simultaneously query the same missing record,
            amplifying the load. Under high inflight work, the discovery cluster struggles to maintain quorum and timely
            responses, observable as increased quorum_lost frequency and a mix of dns_servfail and resolver timeouts.
          flows: [latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain, latency_tracker_dns_servfail, latency_tracker_dns_timeout, ui_widget_ok, ui_widget_backend_503, ui_widget_lookup_failed]
          rate_multipliers:
            latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain: 2.2
            latency_tracker_dns_servfail: 6.0
            latency_tracker_dns_timeout: 5.0
            ui_widget_ok: 0.85
            ui_widget_backend_503: 1.2
            ui_widget_lookup_failed: 1.25
            service_discovery.quorum_lost: 3.0
            backend_api.config_refresh_failed: 1.6
          latency_multipliers:
            ui_widget_backend_503: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.6}
            ui_widget_lookup_failed: {p50: 1.4, p95: 1.8}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation:
            - service_discovery.dns_servfail
            - dns_resolver.upstream_timeout
            - dns_resolver.upstream_timeout_backend_api
            - backend_api.config_refresh_failed
            - web_frontend.access_err

        - order: 3
          at_min: 42
          component: service_discovery
          event: |
            Service discovery operators switch to controlled client admission (rate limiting) and backend teams enable a
            local config fallback, reducing overall query pressure but leaving partial service degradation.
          why: |
            Cutting down the number of admitted clients lowers inflight load and reduces the frequency of quorum loss,
            but dependency/config data is still incomplete for some services and caches must repopulate. The backend’s
            local fallback reduces config refresh failures without fully restoring all lookups.
          flows: [latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain, latency_tracker_dns_servfail, latency_tracker_dns_timeout, ui_widget_ok, ui_widget_backend_503, ui_widget_lookup_failed]
          rate_multipliers:
            latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain: 0.7
            latency_tracker_dns_servfail: 0.8
            latency_tracker_dns_timeout: 0.6
            ui_widget_ok: 1.35
            ui_widget_backend_503: 0.8
            ui_widget_lookup_failed: 0.8
            service_discovery.quorum_lost: 0.8
            backend_api.config_refresh_failed: 0.7
          latency_multipliers:
            ui_widget_ok: {p50: 0.9, p95: 0.9}
          one_shots:
            - ref: service_discovery.client_admission
              count: 1
              hosts: [sd-2]
            - ref: backend_api.fallback_config_enabled
              count: 1
              hosts: [api-1]
          manifestation:
            - service_discovery.client_admission
            - service_discovery.raft_state
            - web_frontend.access_ok
            - web_frontend.access_err

      steady:
        - component: service_discovery
          condition: |
            Cluster remains partially unstable: occasional quorum loss persists and DNS answers intermittently degrade
            (NXDOMAIN for missing latency-tracker record plus periods of SERVFAIL/slow responses under residual load).
          user_impact: |
            Any service relying on discovery/config can intermittently fail to resolve dependencies or refresh runtime
            configuration, contributing to user-facing errors and slow responses.
          manifestation: [service_discovery.quorum_lost, service_discovery.raft_state, service_discovery.dns_servfail]
        - component: web_frontend
          condition: |
            Web/API remains intermittently available with elevated 5xx due to lookup failures and backend 503s, though
            some requests succeed after load control measures.
          user_impact: |
            Dashboards and UI widgets often fail to load or refresh, and API calls intermittently return 5xx.
          manifestation: [web_frontend.dep_lookup_failed, web_frontend.access_err, web_frontend.access_ok]
        - component: backend_api
          condition: |
            Runtime config refresh remains error-prone but is partially mitigated by local fallback; request failures
            still occur when dependencies cannot be resolved.
          user_impact: |
            Even when the UI is reachable, some widget queries fail with 503 and higher latency.
          manifestation: [backend_api.config_refresh_failed, backend_api.req_err]
        - component: intake_cluster
          condition: |
            Telemetry ingestion stays operational; latency tracking is frequently skipped due to DNS instability.
          user_impact: |
            Customers’ incoming telemetry continues to be ingested, but internal latency-tracking-derived signals may be
            missing or delayed.
          manifestation: [intake_cluster.payload_accepted, intake_cluster.tracking_skipped]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: nxdomain_uncached_thundering_herd
          desc: |
            Missing latency-tracker record yields NXDOMAIN that is not cached by resolvers; clients rapidly repeat DNS
            queries, increasing discovery load. Increased load causes slower responses/SERVFAIL/timeouts, which further
            increases client retry/refresh pressure and prolongs instability.

      flows:
        - ui_widget_ok
        - ui_widget_backend_503
        - ui_widget_lookup_failed
        - payload_ingest
        - latency_tracker_dns_nxdomain
        - latency_tracker_dns_servfail
        - latency_tracker_dns_timeout
      manifestation:
        - dns_resolver.cache_miss_nxdomain
        - dns_resolver.upstream_timeout
        - dns_resolver.upstream_timeout_backend_api
        - service_discovery.quorum_lost
        - backend_api.config_refresh_failed
        - web_frontend.access_err

  assumptions:
    - |
      The real ~10-hour outage is time-compressed into 50 minutes while preserving progression: initial NXDOMAIN spike,
      herd expansion after cache expiry, then partial mitigation via controlled client admission and local fallback.
    - |
      Normal phase (0-20m) rough volume: ~1,000 logs/min (~20k total), driven by web (~540/min), ingest (~220/min), and
      cached latency-tracker DNS lookups (~240/min), plus minimal background health logs.
    - |
      Failure phase piecewise volume estimate:
      - 20-30m (10m): ~1,700–2,100 logs/min (NXDOMAIN storm active; SERVFAIL/timeout suppressed) => ~17k–21k logs.
      - 30-42m (12m): ~2,600–3,000 logs/min (SERVFAIL/timeouts appear; web errors worsen; config refresh failures increase)
        => ~31k–36k logs.
      - 42-50m (8m): ~1,100–1,400 logs/min (rate limiting + fallback reduce DNS pressure and config errors) => ~9k–11k logs.
      Total scenario expected: ~70k–90k logs, within the 20k..100k target.
    - |
      Visibility rationale: the root cause is diagnosable from the co-occurrence of high-rate
      dns_resolver.cache_miss_nxdomain + service_discovery.dns_nxdomain, followed by increased
      service_discovery.quorum_lost and the appearance of dns_servfail + resolver timeouts (both latency-tracker
      lookups and backend-api lookups), correlating with web_frontend access_err and backend_api config_refresh_failed.
```
