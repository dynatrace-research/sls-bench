scenario:
  id: hostco_hotwallet_backdoor_apr9
  title: Host wallet backdoor drains funds and triggers maintenance shutdown

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The swap API normally processes client swaps end-to-end, resulting in routine hot-wallet broadcasts.
    During the incident, unauthorized BTC and ETH withdrawals to attacker-controlled addresses begin from a hot wallet host,
    and an SSH login using a compromised ops key fingerprint is observed. Operators place the site into maintenance mode,
    rotate credentials, and begin triage while the attacker later truncates logs and stops log forwarding, causing centralized
    logging to report missing wallet-host logs. After wallet-host logs disappear, continued theft is inferred via blockchain-based
    monitoring alerts rather than local wallet logs; the site remains down through the end of the scenario.

  trigger_desc: |
    A pre-existing backdoor on the hot wallet host is activated using compromised access (stolen SSH key),
    initiating unauthorized BTC/ETH withdrawals; monitoring detects unexpected hot-wallet transfers and the on-call team begins incident response.

  time:
    total_minutes: 32
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 16}
      f: {start_min: 16, end_min: 32}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        The public API serves steady swap traffic. The core accepts swaps and requests withdrawals on the
        hot wallet host, which signs and broadcasts normal transactions.
        Central logging shows stable ingestion and only rare transient gaps.
      flows: [swap_request_success]
      manifestation:
        - api_gateway.http_out_200
        - hot_wallet_server.tx_broadcast_swap
        - log_aggregator.ingest_summary

    f:
      desc: |
        Unauthorized BTC and ETH withdrawals begin from the hot wallet host to attacker-controlled addresses.
        Monitoring flags unexpected hot-wallet transfers, and operators quickly switch the site into maintenance mode
        to stop new swaps and focus on investigation. The attacker intermittently uses a stolen SSH key to
        run privileged commands. Later, cleanup activity truncates logs and stops wallet-host log forwarding; shortly
        after, centralized logging begins issuing sustained missing-source gap warnings and wallet-host logs largely disappear
        from the central log stream. Even with reduced host visibility, blockchain-based monitoring continues to flag
        additional unexpected transfers, implying the drain may be continuing out-of-band from what operators can see on hosts.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 16
          component: hot_wallet_server
          event: |
            The wallet host starts broadcasting BTC and ETH transactions to attacker-controlled addresses, and an SSH login

            on-call enables maintenance mode and declares an incident. No log-wipe activity is observed yet.
          why: |
            The backdoor causes unauthorized withdrawals and uses compromised SSH credentials for access. The attacker destinations
            and key fingerprint are directly observable in wallet host logs; the alerts are directly observable in sec-ops
            logs reflecting monitoring heuristics. The deeper origin of the compromised key is external to this time window.
          flows: [unauthorized_withdrawal, unauthorized_withdrawal_eth, attacker_ssh_session, swap_request_maintenance]
          rate_multipliers:

              count: 1
              hosts: [core-1]

            - hot_wallet_server.tx_broadcast_malware

            - sec_ops.wallet_anomaly_alert
            - sec_ops.wallet_anomaly_alert_eth

          event: |
            Containment actions proceed: operators rotate credentials used for administration and wallet access.
          why: |
            Key rotation is an operational response to suspected credential compromise. It is directly
            observable via operator tooling logs, but may not immediately remove an already-installed backdoor.
          flows: [swap_request_maintenance]
          rate_multipliers: {}

              count: 1
              hosts: [secops-1]
          manifestation:
            - sec_ops.keys_rotated

        - order: 3
          at_min: 22
          component: hot_wallet_server
          event: |
            Cleanup activity begins on the wallet hosts: privileged tampering truncates log files and log forwarding is stopped,

          why: |
            Truncation and forwarding disruption reduce observability. The tampering commands and truncation markers are directly

          flows: [attacker_log_wipe]

            alerts rather than wallet-host transaction logs.

            - sec_ops.wallet_anomaly_alert_eth
            - api_gateway.http_out_503_maint

          event: |
            Forensic triage flags a suspicious binary on a wallet host named udevd-bridge, linking the earlier unauthorized withdrawals
            to a persistent backdoor on the host.
          why: |
            The forensic scan result is directly observable in security tooling logs; determining the original installation path and

          flows: [swap_request_maintenance]

          manifestation:

            Truncated logs and stopped forwarding reduce visibility into attacker actions, slowing containment

          desc: |
            Rotating administrative keys may not stop a backdoor already present on the wallet host, allowing
            continued unauthorized withdrawals that are then inferred primarily through monitoring once host logs are missing.

      log stream; real-world investigation steps (including deeper attribution) occurred over days.
    - >

      - Minutes 16–22: unauthorized_withdrawal ≈ 1 rpm * 6 = 6 rpm (≈ 12 logs/min; 2 logs/flow),

        background health/balance/ingestion ≈ ~7 logs/min,
        sec_ops alerts ≈ (0.35+0.25)/min * 2 ≈ 1.2 logs/min.

    - >
      Total logs estimate: normal ≈ 16 min * (500 rpm * 5 logs) ≈ 40k plus background; failure ≈ 16 min * ~1.5k logs/min
      plus low-rate attacker activity (when visible), gap warnings, and one-shots ≈ 24k–26k; overall ≈ 64k–76k.
    - >
      Visibility rationale: maintenance responses are frequent; early attacker-destination BTC/ETH broadcasts are made likely via
      elevated per-asset withdrawal flows before wallet log forwarding is stopped; the specific SSH key fingerprint appears via
      attacker_ssh_session; after minute 23, wallet-host logs are intentionally suppressed to align with the "missing logs" beat and
      to make log_aggregator.source_gap_warn very likely; continued theft is represented by elevated sec_ops anomaly alerts even when
      host logs are missing; and the malware name becomes visible at minute 26 via a forensic scan one-shot (sec_ops.forensic_scan_found).
