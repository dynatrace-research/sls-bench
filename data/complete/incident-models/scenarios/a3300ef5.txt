```yaml
scenario:
  id: gitlab_com_primary_db_data_loss_20170131
  title: Accidental wipe of PostgreSQL primary data directory during replication repair

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    GitLab.com serves web/API traffic backed by a single PostgreSQL primary with a hot-standby replica.
    A spike in load (spam plus a heavy background user-deletion job) increases DB latency and causes partial write failures.
    Replication then falls behind and breaks due to missing WAL segments; while attempting to rebuild the standby, an engineer accidentally runs a data-directory wipe on the primary.
    The primary cannot start, GitLab.com returns 5xx broadly, and recovery is slowed because logical backups were failing and alerts were not delivered.

  trigger_desc: |
    A sudden increase in database load from concurrent spam traffic and a DB-intensive background job (user removal)
    pushes the primary into high-latency operation, starting the cascade that later leads to replication breakage and a risky manual rebuild.

  time:
    total_minutes: 50
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 25}
      f: {start_min: 25, end_min: 50}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        GitLab.com handles typical read and write traffic successfully. The standby streams normally with low replay lag.
        A scheduled pg_dump backup job runs on an application host; it fails due to a pg_dump/server version mismatch.
        Failure notifications are sent as email but are rejected by DMARC policy, so operators do not notice backup failures.
      flows: [web_read_ok_n, web_post_comment_ok_n, sidekiq_misc_job_n, pg_dump_backup_n]
      manifestation: [gitlab_web.req_done_200, postgres_standby.repl_status, backup_runner.pg_dump_error, mail_relay.dmarc_reject]

    f:
      desc: |
        The failure phase begins with severe DB load that increases request latency and error rates, especially for writes.
        Replication falls behind and then stops because WAL needed by the standby has been removed on the primary.
        Engineers attempt to re-seed the standby and adjust PostgreSQL settings to allow more replication connections, causing restarts.
        During the manual recovery actions, the primary's data directory is accidentally wiped, leaving PostgreSQL unable to start
        and GitLab.com broadly unavailable while operators begin a slow restore-from-staging copy.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 25
          component: postgres_primary
          event: |
            Database load spikes and application requests slow down; write operations begin to fail more frequently.
          why: |
            Increased concurrent activity (spam + DB-heavy background job) elevates DB latency and lock/timeout pressure.
            This is directly observable via higher request durations and more Rails DB exceptions.
          flows: [web_read_ok_f, web_read_5xx_f, web_post_comment_ok_f, web_post_comment_5xx_f, sidekiq_user_destroy_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            web_read_ok_f: 1.0
            web_read_5xx_f: 1.2
            web_post_comment_ok_f: 0.9
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: 1.4
            sidekiq_user_destroy_f: 2.0
            standby_basebackup_attempt_f: 0.0
            postgres_primary.wal_removed: 0.0
            postgres_standby.wal_missing: 0.0
            postgres_primary.startup_panic: 0.0
            ops_bastion.rsync_progress: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            web_read_ok_f: {p50: 2.0, p95: 2.8}
            web_post_comment_ok_f: {p50: 2.3, p95: 3.2}
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: {p50: 1.8, p95: 2.5}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation: [gitlab_web.db_error, gitlab_web.req_done_500, sidekiq_worker.job_done]

        - order: 2
          at_min: 33
          component: postgres_standby
          event: |
            Standby replication falls far behind and then errors because required WAL segments are missing; operators begin repeated pg_basebackup attempts.
          why: |
            Under sustained load, the standby cannot replay WAL quickly enough and the primary removes old segments.
            Without WAL archiving, the standby cannot fetch missing segments and must be re-seeded, prompting pg_basebackup attempts.
            The missing-WAL condition is directly observable on the standby and WAL-removal warnings appear on the primary.
          flows: [standby_basebackup_attempt_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            standby_basebackup_attempt_f: 3.0
            postgres_primary.wal_removed: 1.0
            postgres_standby.wal_missing: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            standby_basebackup_attempt_f: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.8}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_bastion.cmd_exec
              count: 2
              hosts: [bastion-01.gitlab.com]
          manifestation: [postgres_standby.wal_missing, postgres_primary.wal_removed, postgres_standby.basebackup_error]

        - order: 3
          at_min: 38
          component: postgres_primary
          event: |
            Engineers change replication settings and restart PostgreSQL; the first restart fails due to semaphore limits, then succeeds after reducing max_connections.
          why: |
            Increasing max_wal_senders raises resource requirements; with an overly large max_connections value the system hits semaphore limits.
            Operators reduce max_connections and PostgreSQL restarts successfully. This is directly observable via restart and fatal startup logs.
          flows: [web_read_ok_f, web_read_5xx_f, web_post_comment_ok_f, web_post_comment_5xx_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            web_read_ok_f: 0.85
            web_read_5xx_f: 1.6
            web_post_comment_ok_f: 0.7
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: 1.9
          latency_multipliers:
            web_read_5xx_f: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.7}
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.9}
          one_shots:
            - ref: postgres_primary.config_apply_raise_wal
              count: 1
              hosts: [db1.cluster.gitlab.com]
            - ref: postgres_primary.restart_failed_semaphores
              count: 1
              hosts: [db1.cluster.gitlab.com]
            - ref: postgres_primary.config_apply_reduce_conn
              count: 1
              hosts: [db1.cluster.gitlab.com]
            - ref: postgres_primary.server_ready
              count: 1
              hosts: [db1.cluster.gitlab.com]
          manifestation: [postgres_primary.restart_failed_semaphores, postgres_primary.server_ready, gitlab_web.req_done_500]

        - order: 4
          at_min: 43
          component: ops_bastion
          event: |
            A destructive rm command is executed against the primary PostgreSQL data directory, after which PostgreSQL cannot start and GitLab.com shifts to broad 5xx.
            Operators then discover no usable pg_dump backups and begin a slow restore copy from staging.
          why: |
            Manual replication rebuild procedures require wiping the standby data directory; the command is mistakenly run on the primary.
            With critical files removed, PostgreSQL startup fails repeatedly. Backup recovery is blocked because pg_dump backups were failing and notifications were not received.
            The wipe is directly observable via bastion audit logs and repeated PostgreSQL startup failure logs.
          flows: [web_read_ok_f, web_read_5xx_f, web_post_comment_ok_f, web_post_comment_5xx_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            web_read_ok_f: 0.0
            web_read_5xx_f: 2.2
            web_post_comment_ok_f: 0.0
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: 1.8
            pg_dump_backup_f: 0.0
            postgres_primary.startup_panic: 1.0
            postgres_primary.wal_removed: 0.0
            postgres_primary.checkpoint_complete: 0.0
            ops_bastion.rsync_progress: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            web_read_5xx_f: {p50: 1.8, p95: 2.4}
            web_post_comment_5xx_f: {p50: 1.6, p95: 2.3}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_bastion.cmd_exec_rm_primary
              count: 1
              hosts: [bastion-01.gitlab.com]
            - ref: ops_bastion.s3_backup_check_empty
              count: 1
              hosts: [bastion-01.gitlab.com]
            - ref: ops_bastion.rsync_start
              count: 1
              hosts: [bastion-01.gitlab.com]
          manifestation: [ops_bastion.cmd_exec_rm_primary, postgres_primary.startup_panic, ops_bastion.s3_backup_check_empty]

      steady:
        - component: postgres_primary
          condition: |
            PostgreSQL primary cannot start reliably due to missing files in the data directory, emitting repeated startup failures.
          user_impact: |
            GitLab.com cannot access the metadata database; most web/API requests return 500/503 and interactive actions like commenting fail.
          manifestation: [postgres_primary.startup_panic, gitlab_web.req_done_500]
        - component: postgres_standby
          condition: |
            Standby is not usable for failover because replication was broken and operators were attempting re-seeding.
          user_impact: |
            No immediate DB failover path exists; outage persists until a restore can be completed.
          manifestation: [postgres_standby.wal_missing, postgres_standby.basebackup_error]
        - component: backup_runner
          condition: |
            Logical backups are unavailable because pg_dump runs with a mismatched major version and failure notifications are rejected by DMARC;
            operators confirm the backup repository has no recent dumps.
          user_impact: |
            Recovery must rely on an older snapshot copy (larger data loss window) and takes much longer than a standard restore.
          manifestation: [backup_runner.pg_dump_error, mail_relay.dmarc_reject]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: load_to_replication_break
          desc: |
            High DB load increases replication lag; lag makes WAL retention insufficient, causing missing WAL on the standby, which forces risky manual rebuild steps.
        - id: restore_slowness_extends_outage
          desc: |
            With no usable logical backups and no ready replica, restoration depends on copying large datasets over slow storage, prolonging downtime.

      flows: [web_read_ok_f, web_read_5xx_f, web_post_comment_ok_f, web_post_comment_5xx_f, sidekiq_user_destroy_f, standby_basebackup_attempt_f, pg_dump_backup_f]
      manifestation: [ops_bastion.cmd_exec_rm_primary, postgres_primary.startup_panic, gitlab_web.db_error, postgres_standby.wal_missing, backup_runner.pg_dump_error, mail_relay.dmarc_reject]

  assumptions:
    - |
      The scenario compresses multiple real-world hours into 25 minutes of failure while preserving the causal ordering:
      load spike → replication break → manual rebuild actions → accidental primary wipe → slow restore start.
    - |
      pg_basebackup attempts are modeled as a failure-state flow but suppressed at the start of failure (multiplier 0.0)
      and only activated once replication breaks (minute 33) to align with the timeline.
    - |
      Backup failures are made visible via periodic pg_dump attempts (rpm=0.2) plus mail relay DMARC rejection logs emitted in the same window;
      in reality the failures accumulated over days and were not noticed until the incident.
    - |
      After the primary wipe event, DB-dependent success flows are fully suppressed (web_read_ok_f=0.0 and web_post_comment_ok_f=0.0),
      and pg_dump attempts are suppressed (pg_dump_backup_f=0.0) to align with “PostgreSQL cannot start”.
    - |
      Piecewise failure-phase volume/visibility (rough):
      - Minutes 25-33 (8m): elevated latency; ~700-800 rpm across web flows; error-heavy writes; ~1.5k-2.0k logs/min; pg_basebackup suppressed.
      - Minutes 33-38 (5m): replication break visible; pg_basebackup attempts increase; WAL-missing + WAL-removed background logs appear several times.
      - Minutes 38-43 (5m): restart attempts coincide with higher 5xx rates; one-shot config/restart markers appear exactly once each.
      - Minutes 43-50 (7m): near-total outage; web successes suppressed; startup failure logs ~5/min; rsync progress ~4/min after activation; backup check + restore-start one-shots.
    - |
      Total expected log count over 50 minutes is ~75k-90k, dominated by web request logs (2-3 per request) with smaller but decisive operational/DB/replication signals ensuring diagnosability.
```
