scenario:
  id: stackegg_enablement_haproxy_maxconn_outage
  title: StackEgg rollout drives HAProxy to maxconn, causing queued connects and timeouts

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The platform normally serves page views through HAProxy to the web tier with stable concurrency.
    A feature flag rollout enables StackEgg for logged-in users, adding an extra AJAX call for game state after page loads.
    With HTTP keep-alive set to 15 seconds, the added request volume increases concurrent sessions on HAProxy until it hits the configured session cap,
    producing queued TCP connects and client-facing timeouts that are also reflected in external monitoring.

  trigger_desc: |
    StackEgg is enabled network-wide via a feature-flag update, causing logged-in clients to begin calling /stackegg/state on page loads.

  time:
    total_minutes: 40
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 18}
      f: {start_min: 18, end_min: 40}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Users browse normally. HAProxy session counts remain well below the configured cap and connection queuing is absent.
        Page views are served with low latency and monitoring checks remain OK.
      flows: [page_view]
      manifestation:
        - haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k
        - web_app.http_resp_200
        - monitor_pingdom.check_ok

    f:
      desc: |
        After StackEgg is enabled, page loads begin triggering additional asynchronous /stackegg/state requests.
        Because the frontend uses a long HTTP keep-alive (15s), transient requests translate into a larger pool of concurrent sessions.
        HAProxy approaches its maxconn threshold; once reached, new TCP connects are queued, producing highly variable latencies and then client connect timeouts.
        External monitoring flips from OK checks to DOWN checks during the outage window.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 18
          component: feature_flag_db
          event: |
            StackEgg is enabled via a feature flag update; logged-in clients start issuing /stackegg/state AJAX requests after page loads.
            Session counts rise toward the HAProxy maxconn cap, but the frontend is not yet consistently pinned at the cap and widespread client TCP connect timeouts are not present.
          why: |
            The rollout is directly observable via the feature-flag audit log. The resulting extra request stream is visible as StackEgg endpoint
            traffic in web logs, while the initial concurrency rise (below the cap with little queuing) is observable via HAProxy periodic stats snapshots.
          flows: [stackegg_state_ajax, page_view_ok_slow]
          rate_multipliers:
            stackegg_state_ajax: 1.2
            page_view_connect_timeout: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_40k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_60k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k: 1.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k_cap: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_60k: 0.0
            monitor_pingdom.check_fail: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            stackegg_state_ajax: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.3}

              count: 1
              hosts: [db-1]

            - feature_flag_db.flag_update

            - haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k
            - monitor_pingdom.check_ok

        - order: 2
          at_min: 22
          component: haproxy_lb
          event: |
            HAProxy reaches its configured session ceiling and begins queuing new inbound connections.
            Client TCP connect waits become long enough that a portion of requests time out, and monitoring reports the site as down.
          why: |
            The session cap and queuing are observable via HAProxy stats (cur_sess at/near maxconn, queued rising) and via maxconn warnings.
            End-user impact occurs when queued connects exceed client timeouts, producing connect-timeout errors; monitoring shows DOWN checks and opens an alert.
          flows: [page_view_connect_timeout, page_view_ok_slow, stackegg_state_ajax]
          rate_multipliers:
            stackegg_state_ajax: 1.3
            page_view_connect_timeout: 1.6
            page_view_ok_slow: 0.9
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_40k: 1.0
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_60k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k_cap: 1.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_60k: 0.0
            monitor_pingdom.check_ok: 0.0
            monitor_pingdom.check_fail: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            stackegg_state_ajax: {p50: 2.0, p95: 3.0}
            page_view_ok_slow: {p50: 2.5, p95: 3.5}

            - ref: monitor_pingdom.check_alert
              count: 1

            Reduced StackEgg load is observable as a lower rate of /stackegg/state requests and fewer queue warnings; however, keep-alive continues to sustain

          flows: [stackegg_state_ajax, page_view_connect_timeout, page_view_ok_slow]

            stackegg_state_ajax: 0.6
            page_view_connect_timeout: 0.5
            page_view_ok_slow: 1.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k_cap: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.stats_sample_60k: 1.0
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_40k: 0.0
            haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_60k: 0.5
            monitor_pingdom.check_ok: 1.0
            monitor_pingdom.check_fail: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            stackegg_state_ajax: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.8}

        - component: haproxy_lb

            Frontend concurrency remains high relative to normal due to long keep-alive and remaining StackEgg AJAX traffic.

            Most requests succeed but users intermittently see slow loads and occasional connection timeouts, especially during traffic spikes.
            External monitoring is mostly OK after the mitigation, with the outage window having ended.
          manifestation:

      feedback_loops:
        - id: keepalive_amplifies_concurrency
          desc: |
            Long HTTP keep-alive keeps many client connections open, so a short-lived extra AJAX request stream expands into a large pool of
            concurrent sessions; once queuing starts, longer waits further inflate observed concurrency and increase timeout likelihood.

      flows: [page_view_ok_slow, page_view_connect_timeout, stackegg_state_ajax]
      manifestation:
        - haproxy_lb.stats_sample_40k

        - haproxy_lb.maxconn_queue_warn_40k

        - monitor_pingdom.check_fail

  assumptions:
    - >
      The real incident lasted hours with multiple rollout toggles; this scenario compresses to a single
      enablement → maxconn saturation/outage → partial mitigation sequence while keeping the causal core intact.
    - >
      Quantitative scaling: request rpm are scaled down for log volume, and HAProxy cur_sess/queued/maxconn are scaled down to match
      those rpms while preserving the same qualitative behavior (rising concurrency, saturation at cap, then relief after raising the cap).
      Concretely, maxconn=140 represents the real-world ~40k cap and maxconn=210 represents the raised cap (real-world ~60k).
    - >
      To keep maxconn time-consistent (and avoid the raised cap appearing before the mitigation), HAProxy emits separate
      pre-change and post-change stats/queue-warning logs; event 3 switches emission from *_40k to *_60k using rate multipliers.
      Additionally, initial-cap stats are split into pre-saturation (stats_sample_40k) vs at-cap (stats_sample_40k_cap) templates,
      and event 2 switches between them to align with the saturation boundary.
    - >
      HAProxy stats semantics: cur_sess is bounded by maxconn; overload beyond maxconn is represented by queued increasing while cur_sess
      stays at/near the cap.
    - >
      Piecewise failure-phase breakdown (effective rates after multipliers):
      Minutes 18–22 (4m): stackegg_state_ajax ~360 rpm; page_view_ok_slow ~220 rpm; page_view_connect_timeout ~0 rpm;
      stats_sample_40k active (rising, low queued); stats_sample_40k_cap suppressed; monitoring emits OK checks only.
      Minutes 22–30 (8m): stackegg_state_ajax ~390 rpm; page_view_ok_slow ~198 rpm; page_view_connect_timeout ~128 rpm;
      stats_sample_40k_cap active (at-cap, queued); maxconn queue warnings active (~6/min); monitoring emits DOWN checks only.
      Minutes 30–40 (10m): stackegg_state_ajax ~180 rpm; page_view_ok_slow ~220 rpm; page_view_connect_timeout ~40 rpm;
      raised-cap stats active; raised-cap queue warnings reduced (~3/min); monitoring returns to OK checks only.
    - >
      One-shot counts per event: event1=1 (flag update), event2=1 (monitor alert), event3=3 (puppet change + 2 HAProxy reload markers).
    - >
      Normal-phase logs/min estimate: ~1000–1010 logs/min (page_view requests dominate). Failure-phase logs/min estimate: ~1600–2600 logs/min
      depending on the interval (added StackEgg requests and, after saturation, timeout errors), plus ~3–12 background logs/min (HAProxy stats/warnings and monitoring).
    - >
      Total log volume estimate across 40 minutes: ~60k–70k logs. Visibility rationale: per-minute HAProxy stats switching from rising (stats_sample_40k)
      to at-cap (stats_sample_40k_cap) to post-change (stats_sample_60k), activated maxconn queue warnings during saturation, and per-request connect-timeout logs
      plus monitoring DOWN checks/alert make phase boundaries clear without requiring unrealistic concurrency numbers for the scaled traffic.
