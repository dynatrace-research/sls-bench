```yaml
scenario:
  id: use1_internal_edge_congestion_dec2021
  title: Internal/Main network edge congestion causing DNS and EC2 control-plane degradation

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    A control-plane environment relies on an internal network for DNS, monitoring, and EC2 control-plane backends,
    connected to the main network by edge gateway devices. An automated scaling activity triggers an unexpected
    internal-client connection storm, overwhelming the edge and increasing cross-network latency and drops.
    Internal DNS begins returning SERVFAIL and EC2 APIs show elevated 503 timeouts, while monitoring ingestion lags,
    slowing mitigation.

  trigger_desc: |
    An automated capacity scaling activity for a main-network service causes many internal-network clients to
    rapidly open and retry connections through the edge gateway, creating a sustained surge of connection attempts.

  time:
    total_minutes: 34
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 16}
      f: {start_min: 16, end_min: 34}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Cross-network connectivity is healthy: edge gateways show low drop rates and rare conntrack pressure.
        Internal DNS health snapshots show near-zero SERVFAIL, and EC2 control-plane calls succeed with normal latency.
        A small baseline of internal client connection failures occurs but retry backoff is conservative.
      flows:
        - ec2_describe_instances
        - ec2_run_instances
        - internal_dns_query_ok
        - internal_client_connect_ok
        - internal_client_connect_fail
      manifestation:
        - edge_gateway.nat_stats
        - internal_dns.dns_health_normal
        - ec2_api_frontend.http_200_describe
        - monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag

    f:
      desc: |
        The edge gateway becomes congested under a connection storm from internal clients, raising latency and drops
        between the internal and main networks. This degrades foundational services hosted in the internal network:
        internal DNS begins returning SERVFAIL and EC2 control-plane operations increasingly exceed deadlines, producing
        503 responses on customer APIs. Monitoring ingestion lag rises, reducing operator real-time visibility and
        slowing mitigation. DNS is later rerouted away from the congested path, which restores DNS health but leaves
        residual edge congestion and EC2 control-plane timeouts.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 16
          component: autoscale_controller
          event: |
            Automated scaling begins and triggers a surge of internal-client connection attempts; edge gateways show
            increased conntrack pressure and higher queueing/drops, while DNS SERVFAIL and EC2 API 503s become frequent.
          why: |
            A latent client backoff issue causes aggressive retries during the scaling-triggered behavior, amplifying
            connection churn through the internal/main edge devices. This is directly observable via edge_gateway
            pressure logs and rising DNS/API error logs; the precise client bug is partially observable.
          flows:
            - internal_client_connect_fail
            - internal_dns_query_servfail
            - ec2_describe_instances_timeout
            - ec2_run_instances_timeout
          rate_multipliers:
            internal_client_connect_fail: 1.5
            internal_dns_query_servfail: 1.3
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: 1.2
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: 1.2
            edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow: 2.0
            monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag: 2.0
            internal_dns.dns_health_recovered: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.5, p95: 1.4}
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.5, p95: 1.4}
            internal_dns_query_servfail: {p50: 1.4, p95: 1.4}
          one_shots:
            - ref: autoscale_controller.scale_activity_start
              count: 1
              hosts: [autoscale-1]
          manifestation:
            - autoscale_controller.scale_activity_start
            - edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow
            - internal_dns.reply_servfail
            - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_describe
            - monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag

        - order: 2
          at_min: 24
          component: ops_tooling
          event: |
            Operators complete rerouting internal DNS traffic away from the congested edge path; DNS SERVFAILs stop and
            DNS health snapshots return to low error rates, while edge congestion and EC2 API timeouts persist.
          why: |
            Removing a foundational high-volume cross-network dependency (DNS) reduces load on the stressed edge gateways.
            DNS recovery is directly observable via the one-shot change marker and the switch from degraded to recovered
            DNS health snapshots; remaining congestion is observable via continued EC2 503s and edge/monitoring warnings.
          flows:
            - internal_dns_query_ok
            - ec2_describe_instances_timeout
            - ec2_run_instances_timeout
            - internal_client_connect_fail
          rate_multipliers:
            internal_dns_query_servfail: 0.0
            internal_dns_query_ok: 0.2
            internal_dns.dns_health_degraded: 0.0
            internal_dns.dns_health_recovered: 1.0
            internal_client_connect_fail: 1.2
            edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow: 1.2
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: 1.0
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: 1.0
            monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.2}
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.2}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_tooling.dns_reroute_completed
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_tooling.dns_reroute_completed
            - internal_dns.dns_health_recovered
            - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_run
            - edge_gateway.nat_stats

        - order: 3
          at_min: 30
          component: ops_tooling
          event: |
            Additional edge capacity is brought online and the triggering scaling activity is disabled; edge pressure
            and EC2 API timeout volumes decrease, but monitoring ingestion lag remains elevated and RunInstances still
            experiences notable 503 rates.
          why: |
            Adding capacity reduces queueing and conntrack evictions on the edge devices, lowering effective latency and
            drop rates. Disabling the scaling activity reduces ongoing connection churn. Improvements are directly
            observable via lower edge pressure warnings and reduced timeout-flow rates; monitoring recovery is only
            partial because cross-network telemetry remains delayed under residual congestion.
          flows:
            - internal_client_connect_fail
            - ec2_describe_instances_timeout
            - ec2_run_instances_timeout
          rate_multipliers:
            internal_client_connect_fail: 0.7
            edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow: 0.5
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: 0.7
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: 0.8
            monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag: 1.6
            internal_dns.dns_health_recovered: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            ec2_describe_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.15, p95: 1.1}
            ec2_run_instances_timeout: {p50: 1.15, p95: 1.1}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_tooling.capacity_added
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
            - ref: autoscale_controller.scale_activity_disabled
              count: 1
              hosts: [autoscale-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_tooling.capacity_added
            - autoscale_controller.scale_activity_disabled
            - edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow
            - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_describe

      steady:
        - component: internal_dns
          condition: |
            DNS errors have recovered after traffic is rerouted away from the congested path; health snapshots show low SERVFAIL.
          user_impact: |
            Customers and internal services are no longer blocked by internal DNS resolution failures in this region.
          manifestation:
            - internal_dns.dns_health_recovered

        - component: edge_gateway
          condition: |
            Edge gateways remain partially congested: packet drops and queueing are reduced from peak but still elevated,
            and conntrack pressure warnings continue intermittently.
          user_impact: |
            Cross-network dependencies remain slower and less reliable, prolonging control-plane degradation.
          manifestation:
            - edge_gateway.nat_stats
            - edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow

        - component: ec2_api_frontend
          condition: |
            EC2 control-plane requests continue to experience elevated latency and 503 UpstreamTimeouts, with RunInstances
            more impacted than DescribeInstances.
          user_impact: |
            Customers see failures and delays for EC2 APIs, especially for launching new instances and dependent services
            that require new instance provisioning.
          manifestation:
            - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_run
            - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_describe

        - component: monitoring_pipeline
          condition: |
            Monitoring ingestion remains delayed with elevated lag and some dropped samples, limiting operator real-time visibility.
          user_impact: |
            Operators and customers relying on monitoring see delayed metrics, complicating diagnosis and impact assessment.
          manifestation:
            - monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_congestion_loop
          desc: |
            Connection failures/timeouts increase internal-client retries; retries increase connection churn and NAT/conntrack
            pressure on the edge gateway, which further increases latency and failures.

        - id: reduced_visibility_loop
          desc: |
            Monitoring ingestion lag reduces real-time visibility into edge conditions, slowing mitigation and allowing congestion
            to persist longer.

      flows:
        - internal_client_connect_fail
        - internal_client_connect_ok
        - internal_dns_query_servfail
        - ec2_describe_instances_timeout
        - ec2_run_instances_timeout
      manifestation:
        - edge_gateway.conntrack_overflow
        - internal_dns.reply_servfail
        - internal_dns.dns_health_recovered
        - ec2_api_frontend.http_503_describe
        - monitoring_pipeline.metric_ingest_lag

  assumptions:
    - |-
        Timeline compression: minutes 16/24/30 correspond to the real event’s trigger (07:30 PST), DNS reroute completion (09:28 PST),
        and partial congestion relief (early afternoon). The scenario intentionally ends before full regional recovery to keep an end-state
        in failure.
    - |-
        Failure-phase piecewise breakdown (approximate, using effective multipliers):
        - 16–24 (8 min): peak congestion; internal_client_connect_fail ~1.5x, dns SERVFAIL flow ~1.3x, EC2 timeout flows ~1.2x;
          conntrack_overflow and monitoring lag ~2.0x; DNS recovered-health snapshots suppressed.
        - 24–30 (6 min): DNS rerouted; internal_dns_query_servfail suppressed to 0; DNS health switches to recovered snapshots; EC2 timeouts
          persist; monitoring lag remains high; client connection failures still elevated.
        - 30–34 (4 min): added capacity + scaling disabled; client failure and EC2 timeout volumes reduced but not eliminated; monitoring lag
          improves slightly but remains elevated.
    - |-
        One-shots per event: event1=1 (scale start), event2=1 (DNS reroute), event3=2 (capacity added + scaling disabled).
    - |-
        Quantitative alignment note: edge_gateway NAT/conntrack counters are modeled as aggregate device metrics (including unmodeled
        cross-network traffic); their numeric ranges were tightened to avoid orders-of-magnitude conflicts with the modeled flow RPMs
        while still showing a clear spike at failure onset.
    - |-
        Visibility rationale: each event has at least one high-likelihood marker (one-shot change log and/or elevated background warning),
        while the steady-state failure remains diagnosable via continued EC2 503s plus edge/monitoring warnings; DNS recovery is visible via
        the switch to internal_dns.dns_health_recovered and the absence of internal_dns.reply_servfail after event2.
    - |-
        Total estimated log volume across 34 minutes: ~55k–70k logs (within 20k–100k), dominated by request-flow emissions (DNS and EC2)
        and internal-client connection failures with retries, with edge/monitoring background logs providing corroborating time-series signals.
```
