scenario:
  id: us_east_1_mass_reboot_20140527
  title: Accidental mass reboot causes boot storm and delayed control-plane recovery

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The data center normally serves customer API traffic and orchestrates a compute fleet that PXE-boots via an internal DHCP/TFTP service.
    An operator mistakenly targeted all servers with a reboot command, causing simultaneous reboots of API systems and customer compute nodes.
    The resulting boot storm overloaded boot infrastructure, and a subset of legacy nodes intermittently failed DHCP lease acquisition, requiring manual reboots.
    Control-plane stateful components failed to automatically re-form quorum after a full reboot, leaving the API mostly unavailable during the modeled window.

  trigger_desc: |
    Operator uses remote tooling to reboot a small batch of new capacity but mistypes the target selector, expanding it to all servers in us-east-1 and dispatching a mass reboot.

  time:
    total_minutes: 50
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 25}
      f: {start_min: 25, end_min: 50}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Customers make steady API requests and receive normal 200 responses.
        The quorum store emits routine health metrics and the boot service handles only occasional PXE boots.
        Compute node agents emit heartbeats at a stable rate.
      flows: [api_requests_ok, pxe_boot_normal]
      manifestation: [sdc_api.http_200_access, control_store.store_metrics, boot_service.tftp_metrics, node_agent.heartbeat]

    f:
      desc: |
        Immediately after the mass reboot, API hosts and stateful control-plane components restart together and cannot promptly re-establish safe quorum.
        Compute nodes simultaneously attempt PXE boot, creating contention on DHCP/TFTP infrastructure and triggering throttling and retries.
        After throttles are adjusted, many nodes return, but a subset of legacy NIC nodes shows intermittent DHCP lease failures that require additional operator-driven reboots.
        Manual control-plane recovery begins late in the window, allowing a small fraction of API requests to succeed, while most requests still fail.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 25
          component: ops_tool
          event: |
            A reboot command expands to the entire availability zone and is dispatched, causing a simultaneous reboot of API systems and compute nodes.
          why: |
            The remote tooling expands a broad selector without sufficient confirmation and issues immediate reboots to all matched servers.
            This is directly observable via ops_tool logs; the resulting API failures, quorum loss, and PXE boot storm are observable via service logs.
          flows: [api_requests_503, pxe_boot_success, pxe_boot_throttled]
          rate_multipliers:
            api_requests_partial_200: 0.0
            legacy_nic_dhcp_fail: 0.0
            control_store.quorum_lost: 6.0
            control_store.election: 3.0
            sdc_api.startup_wait: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            pxe_boot_success: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.6}
            pxe_boot_throttled: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_tool.cmd_invoke
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
            - ref: ops_tool.target_expanded
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
            - ref: ops_tool.reboot_dispatched
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
          manifestation: [ops_tool.reboot_dispatched, sdc_api.http_503_access, control_store.quorum_lost, boot_service.throttle_drop]

        - order: 2
          at_min: 30
          component: boot_service
          event: |
            Operators adjust boot-service throttles to increase DHCP/TFTP throughput; throttling decreases and successful PXE boots accelerate.
          why: |
            With a simultaneous reboot, the boot infrastructure becomes the bottleneck; relaxing throttles increases concurrency at the cost of higher load.
            This is directly observable via a throttle_update marker and changes in PXE success vs throttling signals.
          flows: [pxe_boot_success, pxe_boot_throttled]
          rate_multipliers:
            pxe_boot_throttled: 0.3
            pxe_boot_success: 1.5
          latency_multipliers:
            pxe_boot_success: {p50: 0.8, p95: 0.7}
            pxe_boot_throttled: {p50: 0.9, p95: 0.8}
          one_shots:
            - ref: boot_service.throttle_update
              count: 1
              hosts: [boot1]
          manifestation: [boot_service.throttle_update, boot_service.tftp_metrics, node_agent.boot_complete]

        - order: 3
          at_min: 38
          component: node_agent
          event: |
            A small subset of rebooted nodes is observed failing to obtain DHCP leases on boot (legacy NIC behavior), prompting targeted reboots of lagging nodes and increasing additional PXE attempts.
          why: |
            Some legacy hardware intermittently fails DHCP at boot, which is observable as DHCP no-offer at the boot service and dhcp_lease_failed logs on affected nodes.
            Operators mitigate by rebooting those nodes to retry boot, adding additional PXE sessions on top of the still-elevated boot demand.
          flows: [legacy_nic_dhcp_fail, pxe_boot_success]
          rate_multipliers:
            legacy_nic_dhcp_fail: 1.0
            pxe_boot_success: 1.8
          latency_multipliers:
            pxe_boot_success: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_tool.cmd_invoke_targeted
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
            - ref: ops_tool.target_expanded_targeted
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
            - ref: ops_tool.reboot_dispatched_targeted
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
          manifestation: [boot_service.dhcp_no_offer, node_agent.dhcp_lease_failed, ops_tool.reboot_dispatched_targeted]

        - order: 4
          at_min: 44
          component: control_store
          event: |
            Manual recovery actions are applied to the control-plane quorum store; API hosts restart and a small fraction of API requests begin to succeed.
          why: |
            After a full reboot, the quorum store blocks automatic recovery without sufficient history to safely elect a leader.
            Manual actions (reseed/force leader) reduce election churn and the frequency of quorum-loss conditions, enabling limited API functionality; this is observable via manual_recover and a new trickle of 200 access logs.
          flows: [api_requests_partial_200, api_requests_503]
          rate_multipliers:
            api_requests_partial_200: 1.0
            api_requests_503: 0.8
            control_store.election: 0.5
            control_store.quorum_lost: 0.4
            control_store.recovery_blocked: 0.6
            sdc_api.startup_wait: 0.7
          latency_multipliers:
            api_requests_partial_200: {p50: 1.8, p95: 2.2}
          one_shots:
            - ref: control_store.manual_recover
              count: 1
              hosts: [cs1]
            - ref: sdc_api.restart
              count: 1
              hosts: [api1]
          manifestation: [control_store.manual_recover, sdc_api.http_200_access, control_store.recovery_blocked]

      steady:
        - component: boot_service
          condition: |
            Boot throughput is improved after throttle changes, but retries still occur under elevated demand and some nodes continue to cycle due to targeted reboots.
          user_impact: |
            Most instances return, but a minority remain offline longer while their hosts retry PXE/DHCP or await operator attention.
          manifestation: [boot_service.tftp_metrics, node_agent.pxe_retry, node_agent.boot_complete]
        - component: sdc_api
          condition: |
            Control-plane quorum is only partially restored; the API serves a small fraction of requests while many still fail with 503 during ongoing recovery.
          user_impact: |
            Customer automation and API-driven operations are largely blocked; some requests succeed intermittently but the API is not reliably available.
          manifestation: [sdc_api.http_503_access, sdc_api.http_200_access, control_store.recovery_blocked, sdc_api.startup_wait]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: boot_storm_contention
          desc: |
            Simultaneous reboots create a PXE request surge that increases boot-service contention; slower boots keep more nodes in retrying state,
            sustaining elevated demand until throughput is increased or the fleet finishes booting.
        - id: quorum_safe_mode_delay
          desc: |
            The quorum store prefers safety (no split-brain) and blocks leader formation without sufficient history after a full reboot,
            prolonging API unavailability until manual recovery actions are applied.

      flows: [api_requests_503, api_requests_partial_200, pxe_boot_success, pxe_boot_throttled, legacy_nic_dhcp_fail]
      manifestation: [ops_tool.reboot_dispatched, boot_service.throttle_drop, boot_service.tftp_metrics, node_agent.pxe_retry, boot_service.dhcp_no_offer, control_store.quorum_lost, sdc_api.http_503_access]

  assumptions:
    - "Compute-node identity is consistently modeled as cn001..cn030 across node_agent.hosts and all node={node} log fields so per-host emissions are correlatable."
    - "The modeled timeline is truncated to a 50-minute window ending before full restoration; it intentionally ends in a still-failing/degraded state per scenario requirements."
    - "Fleet sizing is simplified: 30 compute nodes are explicitly modeled; PXE flow rates represent aggregate per-node PXE boot sessions/attempts rather than packet-level TFTP transfers."
    - |-
      Failure-phase intervals and rough effective volumes:
      - Minutes 25-30 (5m): high quorum churn and elevated PXE throttling (pxe_boot_throttled at 1.0; legacy_nic_dhcp_fail suppressed).
      - Minutes 30-38 (8m): throttles adjusted (pxe_boot_throttled *0.3; pxe_boot_success *1.5).
      - Minutes 38-44 (6m): legacy DHCP issue becomes visible (legacy_nic_dhcp_fail enabled) and targeted reboots add extra PXE load (pxe_boot_success *1.8).
      - Minutes 44-50 (6m): manual quorum recovery starts; quorum-loss frequency drops (control_store.quorum_lost *0.4), enabling api_requests_partial_200 while api_requests_503 remains dominant.
    - "One-shot counts: event1=3 (mass reboot), event2=1 (throttle update), event3=3 (targeted reboots), event4=2 (manual recover + API restart)."
    - |-
      Total log volume estimate:
      - Normal (25m): api_requests_ok ~900 rpm * 25 ~= 22.5k logs plus background (~0.5k) ~= ~23k.
      - Failure (25m): api_requests_503 ~650 rpm * 25 ~= 16.3k; plus partial_200 (~0.4k late), control-plane/boot/node background (~0.6k), PXE flows+retries (~0.2k), and one-shots (~9) ~= ~17k–19k.
      - Total ~= ~40k–45k logs, within the 20k–100k target.
    - "Visibility rationale: ops_tool mass vs targeted one-shots disambiguate the trigger from later mitigation; boot_service.throttle_drop and node_agent.pxe_retry show early boot contention; boot_service.dhcp_no_offer + node_agent.dhcp_lease_failed appear only after event 3 to model delayed diagnosis; and control_store.recovery_blocked plus reduced (but non-zero) control_store.quorum_lost alongside sdc_api.startup_wait make the extended API outage and partial recovery diagnosable."
