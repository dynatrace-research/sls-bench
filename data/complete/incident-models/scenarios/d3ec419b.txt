scenario:
  id: consul_streaming_boltdb_outage
  title: Consul watcher streaming stalls followed by slow-leader instability from Raft log store overhead

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    A microservices stack depends on a single Consul cluster for discovery, KV, and coordination, with Vault and Nomad layered on top.
    Under unusually high read+write churn, enabling Consul's streaming-based watchers produces severe watch delivery lag and pushes KV apply latency into seconds.
    Operators attempt to recover by blocking traffic and restoring from a snapshot, but the cluster degrades again once internal service load returns.
    Disabling streaming restores much of Consul's write throughput and many internal operations begin succeeding again, yet intermittent slow Raft leaders
    associated with Raft log store overhead reintroduce stalls and keep the platform in an outage/maintenance state.

  trigger_desc: |
    A configuration rollout enables streaming-based Consul watchers for a high-churn routing workload, increasing concurrent streams while writes remain high.
    Under these combined conditions, server-side watch delivery falls behind and KV/Raft write latency spikes.

  time:
    total_minutes: 30
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 15}
      f: {start_min: 15, end_min: 30}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Players can join and start sessions through the edge gateway. The game service uses Consul to discover dependencies and uses Vault
        to read auth/session secrets. Internal health checks update Consul KV at a steady cadence and Nomad scheduling is routine. Consul
        periodic metrics show low KV apply latency and stable Raft commit times.
      flows: [player_login, healthcheck_kv_put_ok, nomad_schedule_ok]
      manifestation:
        - consul_cluster.kv_metric_ok
        - edge_gateway.resp_ok
        - game_service.consul_lookup_ok
        - vault.resp_ok

    f:
      desc: |
        Consul becomes unhealthy and acts as a single point of failure: service discovery and health check KV writes time out, Vault storage
        requests slow down or fail, and Nomad cannot reliably coordinate. An attempted snapshot restore with iptables blocking temporarily
        reduces pressure but does not address the underlying watch-delivery stalls; load reintroduction causes degradation again. Disabling
        streaming later improves KV apply latency and allows many internal writes/reads to succeed, but a remaining slow-leader condition
        (later attributed to BoltDB behavior in the Raft log store) causes renewed stalls by the end of the scenario.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 15
          component: ops_control
          event: |
            Streaming-based watchers are enabled for a high-churn routing workload, and Consul KV/Raft latency rapidly degrades under load.
          why: |
            With many watchers and frequent updates, the server falls behind delivering watch updates and internal work queues back up, which
            correlates with KV apply delays and client timeouts. This is directly observable via degraded KV latency metrics and watch delivery lag;
            the deeper internal mechanism is not obvious without profiling.
          flows: [player_login_fail, healthcheck_kv_put_timeout, vault_read_secret_fail, maintenance_page]
          rate_multipliers:
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_ok: 0.0
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad: 1.0

            consul_cluster.raft_log_store_pressure: 0.0
            healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover: 0.0
            vault_read_secret_ok: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            player_login_fail: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
            vault_read_secret_fail: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.streaming_enable
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.streaming_enable
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad
            - consul_cluster.watch_delivery_stall
            - game_service.consul_lookup_fail
            - telemetry_collector.discovery_fail

        - order: 2
          at_min: 19
          component: ops_control
          event: |
            Operators block traffic to Consul with iptables and restore Consul state from a snapshot to attempt a controlled restart.
          why: |
            Blocking access reduces reconnect storms and write pressure while restoring from a known-good snapshot. This is directly observable
            through iptables/snapshot action markers and an immediate drop in Consul-driven request flows, but it does not fix the underlying
            performance problem when traffic is later reintroduced.
          flows: [healthcheck_kv_put_timeout, maintenance_page]
          rate_multipliers:
            player_login_fail: 0.0
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: 0.2
            vault_read_secret_fail: 0.3
            telemetry_collector.discovery_fail: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: {p50: 0.85, p95: 0.95}
            vault_read_secret_fail: {p50: 0.85, p95: 0.95}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.iptables_block
              count: 2
              hosts: [ops-1]
            - ref: ops_control.consul_snapshot_restore
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.iptables_block
            - ops_control.consul_snapshot_restore
            - telemetry_collector.discovery_fail
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad

        - order: 3
          at_min: 23
          component: ops_control
          event: |
            The iptables block is removed to reintroduce internal service traffic; Consul performance degrades again under returning load.
          why: |
            Internal services resume discovery/health writes immediately after the block is lifted. Because the underlying backlog condition remains,
            KV latency stays high and timeouts return. This is directly observable as rising healthcheck timeouts and persistent degraded KV metrics.
          flows: [healthcheck_kv_put_timeout, vault_read_secret_fail, maintenance_page]
          rate_multipliers:
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: 1.0
            vault_read_secret_fail: 1.0
            telemetry_collector.discovery_fail: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
            vault_read_secret_fail: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.iptables_unblock
              count: 2
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.iptables_unblock
            - consul_cluster.watch_delivery_stall
            - game_service.health_put_timeout
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad

          event: |
            Operators disable Consul streaming; KV apply latency drops toward normal and a majority of internal KV writes and Vault reads begin succeeding again,
            while the platform remains in maintenance as recovery continues cautiously.
          why: |
            Disabling streaming changes how watchers are serviced and reduces the server-side backlog, improving write progress.
            This is directly observable via the switch to healthy KV latency metrics and the reappearance of successful healthcheck commits/Vault reads.
          flows: [healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover, vault_read_secret_ok, healthcheck_kv_put_timeout, vault_read_secret_fail, maintenance_page]
          rate_multipliers:
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_ok: 1.0
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad: 0.0
            consul_cluster.watch_delivery_stall: 0.1
            telemetry_collector.discovery_fail: 0.1
            telemetry_collector.scrape_ok: 2.0
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: 0.15
            vault_read_secret_fail: 0.25
            healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover: 1.0
            vault_read_secret_ok: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: {p50: 0.7, p95: 0.85}
            vault_read_secret_fail: {p50: 0.75, p95: 0.9}
            healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover: {p50: 0.9, p95: 1.0}
            vault_read_secret_ok: {p50: 0.9, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.streaming_disable
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.streaming_disable
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_ok
            - game_service.health_put_ok
            - vault.resp_ok
            - telemetry_collector.scrape_ok

        - order: 5
          at_min: 28
          component: ops_control
          event: |
            Slow Raft appends begin appearing on elected leaders; operators force a leader stepdown as leader churn increases and KV/Raft latency worsens again.
          why: |
            A remaining storage/replication bottleneck causes some leaders to append and replicate Raft logs slowly, which surfaces as TCP backpressure and
            stalls seen by clients. This is partially observable via raft-append slow warnings and Raft log store pressure stats; the deeper file-format
            mechanism (later traced to BoltDB freelist behavior) typically requires deeper post-incident analysis/tooling.
          flows: [healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover, vault_read_secret_ok, healthcheck_kv_put_timeout, vault_read_secret_fail, maintenance_page]
          rate_multipliers:
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_ok: 0.0
            consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad: 0.8
            consul_cluster.raft_append_slow: 1.0
            consul_cluster.raft_log_store_pressure: 1.0
            consul_cluster.raft_leader_change: 2.0
            telemetry_collector.discovery_fail: 0.6
            telemetry_collector.alert_gap: 1.5
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: 0.4
            vault_read_secret_fail: 0.7
            healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover: 0.6
            vault_read_secret_ok: 0.5
          latency_multipliers:
            healthcheck_kv_put_timeout: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
            vault_read_secret_fail: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
            healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover: {p50: 1.05, p95: 1.2}
            vault_read_secret_ok: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.3}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.leader_stepdown
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.leader_stepdown
            - consul_cluster.raft_append_slow
            - consul_cluster.raft_log_store_pressure
            - consul_cluster.raft_leader_change
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad

      steady:
        - component: consul_cluster
          condition: |
            Streaming remains disabled, but intermittent slow Raft leaders and leader churn persist alongside Raft log store pressure,
            keeping KV/Raft latency elevated.
          user_impact: |
            Platform bootstrapping remains unsafe: discovery/health writes and coordination intermittently stall, preventing broad service recovery.
          manifestation:
            - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad
            - consul_cluster.raft_append_slow
            - consul_cluster.raft_log_store_pressure
            - consul_cluster.raft_leader_change
        - component: vault
          condition: |
            Vault requests intermittently fail when Consul-backed storage operations stall behind slow Raft appends, even though many reads succeed.
          user_impact: |
            Services that require secrets/tokens cannot authenticate reliably, contributing to continued outage.
          manifestation:
            - vault.resp_ok
            - vault.storage_latency
            - vault.resp_fail
        - component: telemetry_collector
          condition: |
            Telemetry/alerting partially recovers after streaming is disabled, but discovery and alert delivery degrade again during slow-leader periods.
          user_impact: |
            Operators have reduced visibility and delayed alerts while trying to diagnose and recover.
          manifestation:
            - telemetry_collector.discovery_fail
            - telemetry_collector.alert_gap
            - telemetry_collector.scrape_ok

      feedback_loops:
        - id: reconnect_storm
          desc: |
            As Consul degrades, more services retry discovery/health operations, increasing read+write pressure and prolonging the backlog condition.
        - id: observability_dependency
          desc: |
            Monitoring relies on Consul-based discovery; when Consul is unhealthy, fewer diagnostics/alerts are available, slowing triage.

      flows:
        - maintenance_page
        - player_login_fail
        - healthcheck_kv_put_timeout
        - healthcheck_kv_put_ok_recover
        - vault_read_secret_fail
        - vault_read_secret_ok
        - nomad_schedule_fail
      manifestation:
        - consul_cluster.kv_metric_bad

        - consul_cluster.raft_log_store_pressure

        - game_service.health_put_ok

  assumptions:
    - |
      The 73-hour outage is represented as a 30-minute compressed incident with the same ordering:
      watch/stream-related stalls → failed recovery attempts → streaming disabled (partial internal recovery) → remaining slow-leader pathology.
    - |
      Latency multipliers were constrained so that hinted p95 latencies for timeout-style flows do not exceed the modeled timeout-style field caps
      (e.g., waited_ms and edge dur_ms domains capped at 6000ms), improving narrative/control coherence for log generation.
    - |
      External player traffic is represented primarily by maintenance_page; a smaller set of player_login_fail exists early in failure but is
      driven to ~0 once external access is blocked during recovery actions.
    - |
      Failure-phase piecewise volume estimate (rough, after multipliers; excludes small background noise):
      - 15-19 (4m): ~1.45k logs/min + 1 streaming_enable one-shot.
      - 19-23 (4m): ~0.45k logs/min + 2 iptables_block one-shots + 1 snapshot_restore one-shot.
      - 23-26 (3m): ~1.15k logs/min + 2 iptables_unblock one-shots.
      - 26-28 (2m): ~0.93k logs/min + 1 streaming_disable one-shot (timeouts drop; ok flows appear; kv_metric_ok replaces kv_metric_bad).
      - 28-30 (2m): ~0.97k logs/min + 1 leader_stepdown one-shot (slow-leader symptoms appear; kv_metric_bad returns).
    - |
      Normal-phase volume estimate is ~1.5k logs/min for 15 minutes. Total scenario volume estimate is ~40k–45k logs,
      within the 20k..100k target.
    - |
      Visibility rationale: kv_metric_bad and watch_delivery_stall appear repeatedly early; ops one-shots timestamp recovery actions;
      the post-change appearance of health_put_ok and vault.resp_ok makes the streaming mitigation visible; later raft_log_store_pressure and
      raft_append_slow plus increased raft_leader_change distinguish the remaining storage-related instability; telemetry discovery_fail/alert_gap
      provide evidence of the observability dependency on Consul.
