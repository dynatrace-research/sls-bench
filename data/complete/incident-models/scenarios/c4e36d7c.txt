```yaml
scenario:
  id: ops_6000_popcount_retry_storm
  title: "OPS-6000: Popcount overload triggers retry storm and Accesspoint I/O collapse"

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The system streams music and serves discovery content to desktop clients through regional Accesspoints.
    A Discovery rollout introduces a new Bartender→Popcount dependency and increases Popcount traffic beyond its capacity.
    As Popcount slows, a legacy desktop-client Popcount fetch path retries timeouts with no backoff, and Accesspoints emit
    very verbose timeout logs; this combination causes request queues to grow, disk I/O stalls, and widespread Accesspoint
    unreachability. Users see high playback start latency and intermittent inability to log in.

  trigger_desc: |
    A feature rollout enables a Discovery page change where Bartender (Discovery service) fetches playlist subscriber
    counts from Popcount for a slice of European traffic. This newly introduced dependency increases Popcount call volume
    abruptly on a busy Saturday night, before Popcount capacity has been adjusted for the additional load.

  time:
    total_minutes: 36
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 18}
      f: {start_min: 18, end_min: 36}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        During normal operation, desktop clients connect to regional Accesspoints for login, discovery page loads, and
        playback start. Accesspoints proxy login requests to Auth Service (auth_login_ok) and playback start requests to
        Playback Service (playback_start_ok). Discovery page loads (discovery_page_ok) are mostly served from Bartender’s
        cache, so Bartender emits fast render logs and only rare dependency warnings. Popcount subscriber lookups
        (popcount_subscribers_ok) are occasional and typically complete quickly, producing normal Popcount success logs
        and Accesspoint 200 responses. Background infrastructure signals such as Accesspoint syslog flush delays and
        Popcount saturation warnings are rare and low-rate.
      flows: [playback_start_ok, auth_login_ok, discovery_page_ok, popcount_subscribers_ok]
      manifestation:
        - accesspoint.playback_resp
        - accesspoint.login_resp
        - accesspoint.discovery_resp
        - accesspoint.popcount_resp

    f:
      desc: |
        The failure begins when the Discovery rollout causes Bartender to fetch playlist subscriber counts from Popcount.
        Popcount is underprovisioned for this added demand and becomes slow, which increases Popcount tail latency and error
        rate. As Popcount calls time out, the desktop client’s legacy Popcount code retries immediately without backoff
        (popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm), amplifying the load and prolonging the slow state. Accesspoints
        accumulate large upstream queues while waiting on Popcount and produce highly verbose timeout logs per attempt,
        pushing syslog and disk flush into I/O stall. Accesspoint responsiveness collapses: many clients see slow or failed
        logins and high playback start latency because requests can’t be processed reliably at the edge. A late mitigation
        blocks Accesspoints off briefly, converting timeouts into connection failures and triggering exponential backoff
        (popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff), which reduces pressure but does not fully restore service by the end of
        the scenario.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 18
          component: bartender
          event: |
            Discovery rollout traffic starts hitting Bartender with playlist cards that require subscriber counts, and
            Bartender begins issuing Popcount calls during discovery renders. Popcount dependency latency warnings rise,
            and discovery requests that need Popcount enrichment begin returning slower responses and occasional errors.
          why: |
            The rollout adds a previously absent Bartender→Popcount synchronous dependency on a hot user path.
            Popcount capacity was sized for prior callers; the new call volume increases concurrent work and tail latency,
            which is directly observable via bartender.popcount_latency_warn and the flow discovery_page_popcount_timeout
            (bartender.popcount_call_failed). The fact that the dependency is new requires external knowledge (rollout/config)
            to confirm, but the onset pattern (dependency warnings + discovery enrichment failures) is observable in logs.
          flows: [discovery_page_popcount_timeout, discovery_page_ok_cached, popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm]
          rate_multipliers:
            accesspoint.io_wait_high: 0.0
            accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow: 0.0
            accesspoint.queue_depth_warn: 0.5
            popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff: 0.0
          manifestation:
            - bartender.popcount_latency_warn
            - bartender.popcount_call_failed
            - popcount.threadpool_saturated

        - order: 2
          at_min: 21
          component: bartender
          event: |
            Popcount becomes consistently slow under load, and direct subscriber fetches begin failing more often.
            An operator disables the Discovery→Popcount enrichment via a feature flag, and the rate of discovery renders
            that attempt Popcount enrichment drops sharply, while Popcount-related failures continue from accumulated edge
            queues and ongoing client traffic.
          why: |
            Once Popcount is saturated, timeouts increase and the desktop client’s legacy timeout-retry path amplifies
            request rate (observable via accesspoint.popcount_timeout_verbose paired with desktop_client.popcount_retry_no_backoff
            in popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm). Disabling the Bartender enrichment removes one synchronous caller,
            which is directly observable via a one-shot bartender.feature_flag_update and a reduced rate of the enrichment
            failure flow (discovery_page_popcount_timeout), but it does not drain existing Accesspoint queues or stop the
            client retry loop quickly. The remaining overload is observable from Popcount saturation/health logs and Accesspoint
            timeout logs; confirming the feature flag’s full rollout/propagation scope requires external knowledge of config/flags.
          flows: [popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm, discovery_page_popcount_timeout, popcount_subscribers_empty_fastfail]
          rate_multipliers:
            popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm: 1.8
            discovery_page_popcount_timeout: 0.2
            popcount_subscribers_empty_fastfail: 0.7
            accesspoint.queue_depth_warn: 1.5
            accesspoint.io_wait_high: 0.0
            accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow: 0.0
            popcount.threadpool_saturated: 2.0
            popcount.health_unhealthy: 1.2
            bartender.popcount_latency_warn: 1.5
            popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff: 0.0
          one_shots:
            - ref: bartender.feature_flag_update
              count: 1
              hosts: ["bartender-01"]
          manifestation:
            - accesspoint.popcount_timeout_verbose
            - desktop_client.popcount_retry_no_backoff
            - popcount.health_unhealthy

        - order: 3
          at_min: 25
          component: accesspoint
          event: |
            Accesspoints begin exhibiting severe I/O symptoms while handling large Popcount queues and emitting verbose timeout
            logs. Many instances crash and are restarted by supervisors; edge endpoints become intermittently unreachable or
            extremely slow, increasing login timeouts and extending playback start latency.
          why: |
            Verbose per-timeout logging creates a high write rate; syslog flush blocks and disk I/O wait rises, which reduces
            the Accesspoint’s ability to accept/process requests, causing more upstream timeouts and more error logs. This is
            directly observable via accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow and accesspoint.io_wait_high increasing alongside
            accesspoint.popcount_timeout_verbose and one-shot accesspoint.process_crash/accesspoint.process_restart.
            The reinforcing effect (I/O stall → slow handling → more timeouts → more logs) is inferred from known logging/I/O
            behavior but strongly suggested by the correlated signals.
          flows: [auth_login_timeout, popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm, playback_start_degraded]
          rate_multipliers:
            popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm: 2.3
            auth_login_timeout: 2.0
            discovery_page_ok_cached: 0.6
            discovery_page_popcount_timeout: 0.1
            popcount_subscribers_empty_fastfail: 0.5
            accesspoint.io_wait_high: 1.0
            accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow: 1.0
            accesspoint.queue_depth_warn: 2.5
            popcount.threadpool_saturated: 3.0
            popcount.health_unhealthy: 1.5
            bartender.popcount_latency_warn: 2.0
            log_collector.disk_sync_warn: 2.0
            popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            playback_start_degraded: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.8}
            auth_login_timeout: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: accesspoint.process_crash
              count: 10
              hosts: ["ap-eu-01", "ap-eu-02", "ap-eu-03", "ap-eu-04", "ap-eu-05", "ap-eu-06"]
            - ref: accesspoint.process_restart
              count: 10
              hosts: ["ap-eu-01", "ap-eu-02", "ap-eu-03", "ap-eu-04", "ap-eu-05", "ap-eu-06"]
          manifestation:
            - accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow
            - accesspoint.io_wait_high
            - accesspoint.popcount_timeout_verbose
            - accesspoint.process_crash

        - order: 4
          at_min: 30
          component: accesspoint
          event: |
            SRE applies temporary firewall blocks and hard-resets the most unresponsive Accesspoints. Client traffic shifts from
            long Popcount timeouts to immediate connection failures, and the client’s exponential backoff is triggered for
            reconnect attempts; load and logging reduce, but the service remains degraded.
          why: |
            Firewalling/unreachability changes the failure mode from “slow timeout” to “fast connection error”.
            That stops the legacy no-backoff timeout retry loop from dominating and activates the client’s backoff path
            (observable via desktop_client.popcount_conn_failed and desktop_client.client_backoff_scheduled in
            popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff). Reduced request concurrency and log write rate lessen syslog flush
            pressure (observable via decreased accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow and accesspoint.io_wait_high), but Popcount and
            Accesspoints do not instantly return to normal due to remaining backlog and continued high overall demand.
          flows: [popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff, popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm, auth_login_timeout]
          rate_multipliers:
            popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm: 0.7
            popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff: 1.0
            auth_login_timeout: 0.7
            playback_start_degraded: 0.95
            discovery_page_ok_cached: 0.8
            discovery_page_popcount_timeout: 0.05
            popcount_subscribers_empty_fastfail: 0.9
            accesspoint.io_wait_high: 0.6
            accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow: 0.6
            accesspoint.queue_depth_warn: 1.3
            popcount.threadpool_saturated: 1.8
            popcount.health_unhealthy: 1.2
            bartender.popcount_latency_warn: 1.2
            log_collector.disk_sync_warn: 1.0
          one_shots:
            - ref: accesspoint.firewall_block_applied
              count: 6
              hosts: ["ap-eu-01", "ap-eu-02", "ap-eu-03", "ap-eu-04", "ap-eu-05", "ap-eu-06"]
            - ref: accesspoint.hard_reset
              count: 2
              hosts: ["ap-eu-02", "ap-eu-05"]
          manifestation:
            - accesspoint.firewall_block_applied
            - desktop_client.popcount_conn_failed
            - desktop_client.client_backoff_scheduled
            - accesspoint.io_wait_high

      steady:
        - component: popcount
          condition: |
            Popcount remains in a saturated/degraded state: worker pools are frequently near capacity and healthchecks
            oscillate between DEGRADED and UNHEALTHY. Many requests are served via fast-fail empty responses, while others
            still time out upstream when concurrency spikes; Discovery enrichment is mostly disabled, but the retry-driven
            backlog keeps load elevated.
          user_impact: |
            Discovery cards and playlist subscriber information may be missing or slow to load; some pages degrade gracefully
            with empty subscriber lists, while others experience long waits or transient errors.
          manifestation:
            - popcount.threadpool_saturated
            - popcount.health_unhealthy
            - popcount.fast_fail_empty

        - component: accesspoint
          condition: |
            Accesspoints continue to show elevated I/O wait and syslog flush latency compared to baseline, with intermittent
            request queue build-up. Some login requests still time out at the edge due to uneven instance health and backlog.
          user_impact: |
            Users experience intermittent inability to log in and sporadic slow API responses, especially during traffic bursts.
          manifestation:
            - accesspoint.io_wait_high
            - accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow
            - accesspoint.login_timeout

        - component: playback_service
          condition: |
            Playback Service is largely healthy, but playback start latency stays high because Accesspoints are slow to proxy
            requests and deliver responses; tail startup times remain elevated.
          user_impact: |
            Music playback starts slowly; some users see long “loading” before audio begins.
          manifestation:
            - playback_service.session_started
            - accesspoint.playback_resp

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_storm_popcount
          desc: |
            Popcount slows → more upstream timeouts → legacy client retries with no backoff → higher request rate to Popcount
            → even slower Popcount. This loop explains the rapid growth of popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm volume.

        - id: log_io_collapse
          desc: |
            Popcount timeouts → verbose per-timeout logging at Accesspoints → syslog flush + disk I/O wait rises →
            Accesspoints process fewer requests and time out more often → even more error logging. This loop sustains the
            Accesspoint unreachability and broadens impact to login and playback.

      flows:
        - playback_start_degraded
        - auth_login_ok
        - auth_login_timeout
        - discovery_page_ok_cached
        - discovery_page_popcount_timeout
        - popcount_subscribers_empty_fastfail
        - popcount_subscribers_timeout_retry_storm
        - popcount_subscribers_conn_refused_backoff

      manifestation:
        - popcount.threadpool_saturated
        - popcount.health_unhealthy
        - bartender.popcount_latency_warn
        - bartender.popcount_call_failed
        - accesspoint.popcount_timeout_verbose
        - accesspoint.syslog_flush_slow
        - accesspoint.io_wait_high
        - accesspoint.login_timeout
        - desktop_client.popcount_retry_no_backoff
        - desktop_client.client_backoff_scheduled

  assumptions:
    - >
      The post-mortem spans hours and includes eventual recovery; this scenario compresses the timeline to 36 minutes and
      ends during mitigation (firewall/backoff active) but before a full return to baseline, to keep a two-phase model
      (normal→failure) without a separate recovery phase.
    - >
      After the feature-flag change, the Discovery→Popcount enrichment is modeled as largely removed (multipliers near-zero),
      while Popcount overload persists due to client retries and accumulated Accesspoint queues; this matches the narrative that
      “removing that dependency” was attempted but did not resolve the incident.
    - >
      Login impact is modeled primarily as Accesspoint edge timeouts (auth_login_timeout) rather than Auth Service failures,
      matching the narrative that Accesspoints became unreachable/slow due to I/O and logging pressure.
    - >
      The retry-storm timeout flow is interpreted as “edge waited on Popcount and timed out”, so the flow’s path includes the
      Popcount hop and the return to the Accesspoint where the terminal timeout log is emitted.
    - >
      Volume estimate summary (matches system description):
      normal ~= 1417 logs/min * 18 min ~= 25,506;
      failure piecewise ~= 5,367 + 8,749 + 12,568 + 9,563 + one-shots(29) ~= 36,277;
      total ~= 61,783 logs. This is intentionally noisier during failure due to retries and verbose timeout logging,
      then partially reduced after firewalling triggers exponential backoff.
```
