scenario:
  id: m365_wan_router_ip_change_outage
  title: WAN router IP change triggers WAN-wide forwarding impairment and intermittent Microsoft 365 timeouts

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    Microsoft 365 traffic normally flows through an edge gateway that resolves internal DNS and connects to backends over a WAN.
    A planned WAN router IP address change triggers WAN-wide adjacency/forwarding table recomputation, temporarily preventing correct packet forwarding.
    Users experience high latency and timeouts (504) and some DNS timeouts (503), and even the service status portal intermittently fails with 504.
    As the WAN partially self-recovers, automated WAN health/traffic-engineering pauses during instability, leaving residual packet loss and intermittent failures.

  trigger_desc: |
    An operator executes a router IP change command that behaves differently across devices and causes the router to send messages to all other WAN routers,
    triggering WAN-wide adjacency and forwarding-table recomputation.

  time:
    total_minutes: 40
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 20}
      f: {start_min: 20, end_min: 40}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        The edge gateway serves Microsoft 365 requests with low latency. For each request it performs internal DNS resolution and reaches the backend over stable WAN paths.
        Synthetic WAN probes from the automation controller report healthy RTT. Router and automation background telemetry are steady and low-noise.
      flows: [m365_http_ok, status_portal_ok, wan_probe_ok]
      manifestation:
        - m365_edge_gateway.resp_200_m365
        - dns_resolver.dns_answer_ok
        - wan_automation_controller.probe_result_ok
        - wan_router_fleet.router_heartbeat

    f:
      desc: |

        During recomputation, forwarding becomes unreliable and causes packet loss and long latency for traffic traversing the WAN, including DNS lookups and edge-to-backend connections.
        User impact occurs in noticeable surges (modeled here as a spike shortly after the trigger), and later the WAN begins to partially recover on its own.
        Automated WAN health and traffic engineering then pause due to the instability, prolonging residual packet loss and intermittent 504/503 responses through the end of this scenario.

        - id: retry_amplifies_instability
          desc: |
            As the edge gateway retries upstream selection during WAN loss, additional connection attempts and retransmissions increase load and variability,
            which can exacerbate queueing and make convergence appear "bursty" (waves of higher timeouts).

        - id: automation_pause_prolongs_loss
          desc: |
            When WAN automation pauses, unhealthy-path elimination and traffic engineering stop adapting, allowing residual loss to persist longer than
            the initial routing recomputation would have on its own.

    - |

      - 34-38 automation paused / residual loss
      - 38-40 partial relief but still failing
    - |
      Approx effective log volume (order-of-magnitude):

      * Failure (20-40): baseline ~1400–1450 logs/min, with multipliers causing ~1300–2100 logs/min depending on segment → ~26k–42k logs.
      * One-shots: router IP-change + recompute start (2), automation paused (1), automation restart (2).
      * Total expected: ~52k–68k logs (within 20k–100k).
    - |
      Visibility rationale: router fwd_drop_metric/routing_recompute_metric appear many times per host during failure; gateway resp_504/resp_503 and dns_timeout
      appear frequently in failure request flows; automation pause/restart are guaranteed via one-shots.
