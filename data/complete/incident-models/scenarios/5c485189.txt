```yaml
scenario:
  id: dns_resolver_stall_spike_outage
  title: Traffic spike triggers DNS connect stalls and widespread 504s

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The hosted package repository normally serves package metadata and artifacts through a load balancer and a small frontend tier.
    A scheduled automation wave causes an extreme traffic spike (~6x baseline), and frontends begin stalling on connect() to the
    datacenter DNS resolver, tying up workers and producing many 503/504 responses. External monitoring detects the outage and pages
    on-call; operator actions confirm the DNS-connect stall, but the incident ends while the service is still degraded.

  trigger_desc: |
    A periodic config-management/automation job across many clients starts simultaneously and generates a synchronized surge of package
    repository requests well above typical spikes.

  time:
    total_minutes: 24
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 12}
      f: {start_min: 12, end_min: 24}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Baseline traffic is steady and served successfully. Load balancer stats show low queueing and near-zero 5xx counts.
        Frontend metrics show low inflight load and fast DNS lookups, and external monitoring checks succeed.
      flows: [fetch_package_n, monitor_check_n]
      manifestation: [edge_lb.access_2xx, edge_lb.lb_stats, app_frontend.app_metrics, external_monitor.check_ok]

    f:
      desc: |
        At failure start, a synchronized automation spike increases request volume dramatically. As the spike continues, the
        datacenter DNS resolver becomes slow/erratic, and frontend workers spend significant time blocked waiting on DNS connect()
        and lookups. The load balancer increasingly returns 504/503 while frontend inflight load and DNS timeout rates climb.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 12
          component: edge_lb
          event: |
            A scheduled client automation wave hits and request volume jumps to ~6x baseline; backend queueing begins and some requests
            start timing out, triggering external monitoring failures and paging.
          why: |
            The frontends handle far more concurrent requests, increasing dependency calls including DNS. Early in the spike,
            contention and dependency latency start pushing responses over timeouts (directly observable via LB 5xx and monitor failures).
          flows: [fetch_package_ok_f, fetch_package_fail_f, monitor_check_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: 1.0
            fetch_package_fail_f: 1.0
            monitor_check_f: 1.0
            app_frontend.dns_timeout: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.4}
            fetch_package_fail_f: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: external_monitor.page_sent
              count: 1
              hosts: [mon-1]
          manifestation: [external_monitor.page_sent, edge_lb.lb_stats, edge_lb.access_5xx, app_frontend.app_metrics]

        - order: 2
          at_min: 15
          component: dns_resolver
          event: |
            Resolver performance degrades under the spike: DNS response times and TCP connection errors increase, and frontend DNS timeout
            warnings become more frequent. The successful share of requests drops while 503/504 responses rise.
          why: |
            With many frontend workers attempting DNS connections/queries concurrently, the shared resolver becomes a bottleneck and may
            shed load or slow down, causing connect()/lookup stalls. This is observable indirectly (frontend DNS warnings/metrics) and
            corroborated by resolver stats; the exact rate-limiting policy is external to the app.
          flows: [fetch_package_ok_f, fetch_package_fail_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: 0.5
            fetch_package_fail_f: 1.5
            app_frontend.dns_timeout: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.0}
            fetch_package_fail_f: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.3}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation: [app_frontend.dns_timeout, app_frontend.app_metrics, dns_resolver.dns_stats, edge_lb.access_5xx]

        - order: 3
          at_min: 18
          component: oncall_ops
          event: |
            On-call increases load balancer weight and shifts capacity toward the frontend tier, but the new frontend remains oddly low
            on CPU; a sampled process shows it blocked in connect() to the DNS resolver.
          why: |
            Adding/weighting frontend capacity does not help when the shared DNS resolver is the bottleneck: more workers still stall on
            the same dependency. The strace sample provides direct evidence of connect() blocking, while LB 5xx and frontend metrics remain elevated.
          flows: [fetch_package_ok_f, fetch_package_fail_f]
          rate_multipliers: {}
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: oncall_ops.lb_weight_change
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
            - ref: oncall_ops.shift_capacity
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
            - ref: oncall_ops.strace_sample
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-1]
          manifestation: [oncall_ops.strace_sample, app_frontend.dns_timeout, edge_lb.access_5xx]

        - order: 4
          at_min: 21
          component: edge_lb
          event: |
            As the spike persists, request queueing worsens and timeouts dominate; most client requests are now served as 504/503.
          why: |
            Frontend workers are increasingly consumed by blocked DNS connect/lookups, reducing effective throughput. More requests exceed
            upstream timeouts and fail at the load balancer (directly observable via LB access 5xx and increasing DNS timeout warnings).
          flows: [fetch_package_ok_f, fetch_package_fail_f, monitor_check_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: 0.2
            fetch_package_fail_f: 1.8
            app_frontend.dns_timeout: 2.5
          latency_multipliers:
            fetch_package_ok_f: {p50: 2.0, p95: 3.0}
            fetch_package_fail_f: {p50: 1.5, p95: 1.8}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation: [edge_lb.lb_stats, edge_lb.access_5xx, app_frontend.app_metrics, external_monitor.check_fail]

      steady:
        - component: app_frontend
          condition: |
            Frontend workers remain saturated with high inflight counts; DNS connect/lookup latency and timeouts stay elevated, keeping
            throughput low even with additional instances present.
          user_impact: |
            Most users experience repository unavailability (timeouts or 503/504) during the spike window.
          manifestation: [app_frontend.app_metrics, app_frontend.dns_timeout]
        - component: edge_lb
          condition: |
            The load balancer continues to return a high fraction of 504/503 due to slow/unresponsive upstreams; backend queue remains high.
          user_impact: |
            Client requests frequently fail fast with 503 or time out to 504; automation may retry and amplify load.
          manifestation: [edge_lb.access_5xx, edge_lb.lb_stats]
        - component: external_monitor
          condition: |
            External checks continue to fail intermittently and report high latency.
          user_impact: |
            Monitoring shows sustained outage conditions; on-call remains engaged.
          manifestation: [external_monitor.check_fail]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: automation_retry_amplification
          desc: |
            Clients performing automation often retry failed package fetches. During the spike this increases concurrent request volume,
            further stressing frontends and the shared DNS resolver, which in turn increases timeouts and prompts more retries.

      flows: [fetch_package_ok_f, fetch_package_fail_f, monitor_check_f]
      manifestation: [edge_lb.access_5xx, edge_lb.lb_stats, app_frontend.dns_timeout, app_frontend.app_metrics, dns_resolver.dns_stats, external_monitor.check_fail]

  assumptions:
    - >
      This scenario models one of the repeated spike-driven outages specifically in the "add capacity but still down" era, where the
      observed bottleneck is DNS connectivity (connect() stalls) rather than a load balancer max-connection cap.
    - >
      Failure-phase piecewise breakdown (12 minutes total):
      minutes 12-15: fetch ok 2400 rpm, fetch fail 2400 rpm, dns_timeout ~90/min total, steady stats/metrics (~110/min).
      minutes 15-18: ok multiplier 0.5 (1200 rpm), fail multiplier 1.5 (3600 rpm), dns_timeout multiplier 2.0 (~180/min total).
      minutes 18-21: rates as above, plus 3 ops one-shots at minute 18.
      minutes 21-24: ok multiplier 0.2 (480 rpm), fail multiplier 1.8 (4320 rpm), dns_timeout multiplier 2.5 (~225/min total).
    - >
      Consistency note: app_frontend.app_metrics.dns_timeouts_1m is intended to be on the same order as emitted WARNs
      app_frontend.dns_timeout per host (rolling 1-minute count), with normal kept near-zero and failure elevated.
    - >
      Visibility rationale: LB access 5xx dominates during failure (tens of thousands of logs), edge_lb.lb_stats cleanly separates
      normal (near-zero 5xx/queue) from failure (elevated), app_frontend.app_metrics emits every 10s per host, and app_frontend.dns_timeout
      is frequent during the spike; together they make the DNS stall progression and impact obvious.
    - >
      Domain alignment note: edge_lb.access_5xx rt_ms/upstream_ms domains are sized to accommodate the largest modeled failure
      latency hint after multipliers (up to ~18000ms implied p95).
    - >
      Estimated total logs: ~69k (normal ~9.9k; failure ~59k including ops one-shots), within the 20k–100k target.
```
