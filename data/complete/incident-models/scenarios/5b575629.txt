scenario:
  id: mcp_bgp_policy_term_reorder_outage
  title: MCP PoP outage due to BGP policy term reordering withdrawing site-local prefixes

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The system models Cloudflare’s MCP PoP request handling: client HTTP traffic reaches an edge proxy,
    which serves from cache when possible and otherwise routes to internal compute clusters and customer
    origins. The incident is triggered by an automated rollout of a spine-router BGP export policy where
    term reordering causes site-local prefixes to be rejected and withdrawn. This breaks internal forwarding
    (Multimog can’t balance across clusters) and impairs origin reachability, producing widespread timeouts,
    retries, overload, and elevated 5xx/52x responses for users whose traffic lands in the affected MCP PoPs.

  trigger_desc: |
    A network configuration rollout to MCP spine routers deployed a change intended to add/standardize BGP
    communities, but during deployment the policy terms were reordered such that the site-local advertisement
    terms were moved below an explicit REJECT-THE-REST term. This external action (a committed configuration
    change pushed by automation) occurred during a staged rollout and reached MCP-enabled locations, causing
    immediate withdrawal of critical site-local prefixes and loss of internal reachability.

  time:
    total_minutes: 40
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 20}
      f: {start_min: 20, end_min: 40}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        MCP PoPs operate normally: edge proxies accept client requests and assign req_id/trace_id
        (edge_proxy.req_received). Most traffic is served from cache (http_cache_hit), producing fast
        responses (edge_proxy.resp_sent with 2xx/3xx/404). Cache misses (http_cache_miss_origin_ok) call
        the origin fetcher, which connects to customer origins and returns successfully
        (origin_fetcher.fetch_success), after which responses are sent to clients.
        
        Spine routers advertise the required internal and external prefixes; periodic BGP summaries
        (spine_router.bgp_summary) show a healthy number of advertised prefixes. NOC monitoring runs
        synthetic probes (edge_healthcheck_ok) that consistently succeed (noc_monitor.http_probe_ok),
        confirming the PoP is healthy and reachable.
      flows: [http_cache_hit, http_cache_miss_origin_ok, edge_healthcheck_ok]
      manifestation:
        - edge_proxy.resp_sent
        - cache_cluster.cache_hit
        - spine_router.bgp_summary
        - noc_monitor.http_probe_ok

    f:
      desc: |
        At failure start, an automated config rollout reaches MCP spine routers and commits a policy update.
        Immediately, the spines begin withdrawing site-local prefixes required for intra-PoP mesh reachability
        and for origin access paths. As these routes disappear, internal forwarding in the Clos mesh degrades:
        cache peers become intermittently unreachable, Multimog cannot forward to backends reliably, and the
        origin fetcher experiences increasing connect and first-byte timeouts.
        
        The edge proxy attempts retries for failing upstream work (edge_proxy.retry_scheduled), amplifying
        load on already impaired internal paths. With Multimog unable to balance traffic across clusters, smaller
        compute clusters see disproportionate load and begin shedding requests (compute_worker.request_rejected_overload),
        further increasing error rates. Monitoring escalates from probe failures to sustained paging
        (noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired). Attempts to revert the change are complicated by intermittent reachability
        and overlapping changes, causing route flaps (spine_router.bgp_session_flap) and renewed withdrawal bursts.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 20
          component: spine_router
          event: |
            A new BGP export-policy commit is applied on MCP spine routers, and withdrawals of site-local
            prefixes begin immediately. Within minutes, origin fetches start timing out and internal load
            balancer forwarding begins failing with "no route" errors.
          why: |
            BGP policy terms are evaluated in order; the commit reorders the site-local advertisement terms
            below an explicit catch-all reject term. As a result, site-local routes match the reject before they
            can be accepted/advertised, and the routers withdraw those prefixes from peers and internal routing.
            This mechanism is partially observable: the effect is directly visible in spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
            and degraded spine_router.bgp_summary values, while the exact term ordering requires configuration
            diff/validation outside these runtime logs.
          flows: [http_origin_timeout, http_internal_lb_fail]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_cache_hit_degraded: 1.2
            http_origin_timeout: 1.0
            http_internal_lb_fail: 0.6
            http_compute_overloaded_503: 0.0
            multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing: 0.5
            noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            http_origin_timeout: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}
          one_shots:
            - ref: config_pusher.rollout_start
              count: 1
            - ref: spine_router.config_commit_applied
              count: 2
            - ref: spine_router.policy_term_order_warning
              count: 2
          manifestation:
            - spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
            - origin_fetcher.fetch_timeout
            - multimog_lb.forward_fail_no_route

        - order: 2
          at_min: 24
          component: multimog_lb
          event: |
            Internal mesh forwarding degrades further: Multimog reports missing mesh routes continuously,
            and compute backends begin rejecting requests due to overload. Monitoring shifts from isolated probe
            failures to sustained alert firing as 5xx/timeout rates rise.
          why: |
            Without the site-local prefixes, Multimog cannot reliably reach many backend destinations and its
            cross-cluster forwarding effectively collapses; traffic that would normally spread across multiple
            compute clusters concentrates on reachable (often smaller) clusters. Those clusters hit queue/CPU
            limits and reject requests, which increases upstream errors and triggers retries from the edge, creating
            more load on failing paths. This is largely observable from multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing,
            compute_worker.request_rejected_overload, edge_proxy.retry_scheduled, and increased http_origin_timeout/
            http_internal_lb_fail flow volume; the precise traffic distribution skew is inferred from symptoms.
          flows: [http_origin_timeout, http_internal_lb_fail, http_compute_overloaded_503, edge_healthcheck_fail]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_cache_hit_degraded: 0.8
            http_origin_timeout: 1.3
            http_internal_lb_fail: 1.5
            http_compute_overloaded_503: 1.0
            edge_healthcheck_fail: 1.2
            multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing: 2.0
            noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            http_origin_timeout: {p50: 2.0, p95: 3.0}
            http_internal_lb_fail: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.8}
          one_shots:
            - ref: noc_monitor.incident_declared
              count: 1
          manifestation:
            - multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing
            - compute_worker.request_rejected_overload
            - noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired

        - order: 3
          at_min: 30
          component: spine_router
          event: |
            Engineers make verification changes on a subset of spines; BGP sessions begin flapping and the
            withdrawal rate briefly decreases before returning. User-facing errors continue, but with short-lived
            partial improvements as some routes intermittently reappear.
          why: |
            Partial reverts and manual policy checks can temporarily restore advertisements on individual routers,
            but inconsistent policy state across spines and peers causes session resets and oscillating route
            propagation. This is directly observable via increases in spine_router.bgp_session_flap and changes
            in spine_router.prefix_withdrawn rate, while the exact operator actions and configuration correctness
            require external operational context beyond the steady-state router logs.
          flows: [http_origin_timeout, http_internal_lb_fail, http_compute_overloaded_503]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_cache_hit_degraded: 1.1
            http_origin_timeout: 1.0
            http_internal_lb_fail: 1.1
            http_compute_overloaded_503: 0.7
            spine_router.prefix_withdrawn: 0.8
            spine_router.bgp_session_flap: 3.0
            multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing: 1.5
            noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            http_origin_timeout: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: config_pusher.rollout_change
              count: 1
          manifestation:
            - spine_router.bgp_session_flap
            - spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
            - edge_proxy.retry_scheduled

        - order: 4
          at_min: 35
          component: config_pusher
          event: |
            Overlapping revert attempts occur and route advertisements oscillate again: withdrawal logs spike,
            probe failures intensify, and origin timeouts rise. The PoP remains degraded through the end of the
            scenario with intermittent brief recoveries.
          why: |
            Multiple concurrent changes to the same routing policy can overwrite prior corrections, reintroducing
            the reject-before-accept behavior and forcing repeated withdrawals. Each withdrawal wave breaks internal
            reachability anew, which increases timeouts and edge retries, reinforcing overload and instability.
            This is partially observable from repeated spikes in spine_router.prefix_withdrawn, higher
            spine_router.bgp_session_flap, and sustained noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired; confirming "change collision"
            typically requires change-management/audit data outside runtime logs.
          flows: [http_origin_timeout, http_internal_lb_fail, http_compute_overloaded_503, edge_healthcheck_fail]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_cache_hit_degraded: 0.9
            http_origin_timeout: 1.4
            http_internal_lb_fail: 1.6
            http_compute_overloaded_503: 1.2
            edge_healthcheck_fail: 1.3
            spine_router.prefix_withdrawn: 1.4
            spine_router.bgp_session_flap: 6.0
            multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing: 2.0
            noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired: 1.5
          latency_multipliers:
            http_origin_timeout: {p50: 2.2, p95: 3.5}
            http_internal_lb_fail: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: spine_router.config_commit_applied
              count: 2
          manifestation:
            - spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
            - noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired
            - origin_fetcher.fetch_timeout

      steady:
        - component: spine_router
          condition: |
            Spine routers continue to withdraw site-local prefixes and experience BGP session flaps, keeping
            internal routes incomplete and unstable. The BGP summary shows a sustained reduction in advertised
            prefixes compared to normal.
          user_impact: |
            Requests that depend on internal forwarding and stable routing fail or time out; affected MCP PoPs
            intermittently become partially functional and then regress.
          manifestation:
            - spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
            - spine_router.bgp_session_flap
            - spine_router.bgp_summary

        - component: multimog_lb
          condition: |
            Multimog’s mesh forwarding remains impaired (missing routes), limiting cross-cluster balancing and
            causing uneven distribution to whatever backends are reachable.
          user_impact: |
            Dynamic requests routed through internal services see elevated 502/503/52x errors and high latency,
            especially during oscillation periods.
          manifestation:
            - multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing
            - multimog_lb.forward_fail_no_route

        - component: edge_proxy
          condition: |
            Edge proxies continue retrying upstream work and emitting upstream errors due to origin timeouts
            and internal no-route failures; a smaller subset of cache hits still succeeds.
          user_impact: |
            End users observe intermittent site failures, slow loads, and error pages; many requests complete
            as 5xx/52x after retries and long timeouts.
          manifestation:
            - edge_proxy.upstream_error
            - edge_proxy.retry_scheduled
            - edge_proxy.resp_sent

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_amplification_overload
          desc: |
            Missing site-local routes cause timeouts/no-route errors → edge retries increase per-user work →
            retries add load to already impaired internal paths and concentrated backends → more overload and
            longer timeouts → even more retries and errors.

      flows:
        - http_cache_hit_degraded
        - http_origin_timeout
        - http_internal_lb_fail
        - http_compute_overloaded_503
        - edge_healthcheck_fail
      manifestation:
        - spine_router.prefix_withdrawn
        - spine_router.bgp_session_flap
        - spine_router.bgp_summary
        - multimog_lb.mesh_route_missing
        - multimog_lb.forward_fail_no_route
        - compute_worker.request_rejected_overload
        - origin_fetcher.fetch_timeout
        - edge_proxy.upstream_error
        - edge_proxy.retry_scheduled
        - noc_monitor.alert_rule_fired
        - noc_monitor.http_probe_fail

  assumptions:
    - >
      The real incident included full recovery; this scenario ends while the system remains degraded
      (still in failure) to fit a two-phase structure with no recovery phase, while still reflecting
      the documented “sporadic re-appearance” due to overlapping reverts.
    - >
      The modeled failure start is aligned to the post-mortem’s “rollout reached MCP spines” moment,
      with subsequent escalation compressed into a 20-minute failure window for the 40-minute scenario.
    - >
      Volume estimate recap: normal phase ~1502 logs/min for 20 min (~30k). Failure phase piecewise:
      [20,24) ~1403/min*4 (~5.6k), [24,30) ~2100/min*6 (~12.6k), [30,35) ~1690/min*5 (~8.5k),
      [35,40) ~2305/min*5 (~11.5k), plus ~10-30 one-shots; total ~68k logs.
    - >
      Flow-rate choices downscale Cloudflare’s global traffic to a tractable synthetic-log volume while
      preserving relative behavior: lower inbound rpm during failure plus higher retry-driven noise, and
      clear diagnostic signals (withdrawals, mesh-route missing, origin timeouts, and alert firing).
