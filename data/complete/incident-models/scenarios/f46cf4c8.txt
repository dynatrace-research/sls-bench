scenario:
  id: atl_backbone_route_leak_overload
  title: Atlanta backbone route leak attracted traffic and overloaded router; later observability drops

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    The edge network normally serves requests locally within POPs, using a private backbone for inter-POP routing when needed.
    A configuration change on the Atlanta backbone router inadvertently makes Atlanta-exported routes overly preferred, attracting traffic from many POPs.
    Atlanta becomes overloaded, affected POPs stop serving traffic locally, and many requests time out or return 5xx while overall traffic drops roughly in half.
    Operations remove Atlanta from the backbone to restore customer traffic, but a central observability pipeline later congests and drops some telemetry.

  trigger_desc: |
    A network engineer commits a backbone routing policy change on the Atlanta router intended to reduce congestion, but the change unintentionally causes a broad iBGP route export with higher local preference.

  time:
    total_minutes: 50
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 25}
      f: {start_min: 25, end_min: 50}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        POPs serve HTTP requests locally with stable latency and low error rates. Backbone routing logs show
        routine summaries, and occasional link congestion warnings may appear without impacting request success.
        The observability pipeline ingests telemetry normally.
      flows: [http_req_affected_ok_n, http_req_unaffected_ok_n]
      manifestation:
        [edge_pop_affected.req_complete_ok, edge_pop_unaffected.req_complete_ok, traffic_manager.pop_sample, backbone_router_atl.bgp_advertise_summary, core_observability.ingest_metric]

    f:
      desc: |
        At failure start, a routing policy change in Atlanta causes traffic from many POPs to prefer Atlanta via the backbone.
        Atlanta's router exports far more attractive routes, peers churn bestpath selection, and Atlanta overload causes packet drops and request failures.
        The edge performs internal upstream retries toward Atlanta, and client requests complete with 5xx/timeouts.
        Mitigation removes Atlanta from the backbone, restoring normal request routing and success, but later the observability ingest pipeline becomes congested and drops telemetry.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 25
          component: backbone_router_atl
          event: |
            A configuration commit on the Atlanta backbone router is followed by a sharp increase in exported prefixes and bestpath changes toward Atlanta, concentrating traffic in Atlanta and driving upstream timeouts/retries and client 5xx from affected POPs while overall request volume drops.
          why: |
            The policy mistake makes many routes more preferred via Atlanta (higher local preference), which is directly observable via the commit marker plus elevated export/bestpath-churn logs; the customer impact is observable via frequent client error completions and upstream-fetch retry markers.
          flows: [http_req_affected_to_atl_fail_f, http_req_affected_client_err_f, http_req_unaffected_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_req_affected_ok_f: 0.0
            http_req_unaffected_ok_f: 0.5
            http_req_affected_client_err_f: 0.25
            http_req_affected_to_atl_fail_f: 0.25
            backbone_router_atl.bgp_advertise_summary: 2.0
            backbone_router_atl.cpu_overload: 1.0
            backbone_router_atl.forwarding_drops: 1.0
            backbone_router_peer.bgp_bestpath_change: 2.0
            backbone_router_atl.participation_status_disabled: 0.0
            backbone_router_peer.neighbor_state_idle: 0.0
            core_observability.drop_warn: 0.0
          latency_multipliers:
            http_req_affected_to_atl_fail_f: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.3}
            http_req_affected_client_err_f: {p50: 1.1, p95: 1.2}
          one_shots:
            - ref: backbone_router_atl.config_commit
              count: 1
              hosts: [atl01]
            - ref: traffic_manager.anomaly_alert
              count: 1
              hosts: [tm01]
          manifestation:
            [backbone_router_atl.config_commit, backbone_router_atl.cpu_overload, backbone_router_peer.bgp_bestpath_change, edge_pop_affected.retrying_upstream, edge_pop_affected.req_complete_err]

        - order: 2
          at_min: 35
          component: backbone_router_atl
          event: |
            Operations disable Atlanta from the backbone; BGP neighbor-down events occur and affected POPs resume local routing with request success returning.
          why: |
            Removing Atlanta from backbone participation withdraws the overly preferred paths and stops the traffic concentration; this is directly observable from the router disable marker and neighbor-down logs, and indirectly from the sharp drop in client error completions and upstream retries.
          flows: [http_req_affected_ok_f, http_req_unaffected_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            http_req_affected_ok_f: 1.0
            http_req_unaffected_ok_f: 1.0
            http_req_affected_client_err_f: 0.02
            http_req_affected_to_atl_fail_f: 0.02
            backbone_router_atl.cpu_overload: 0.0
            backbone_router_atl.forwarding_drops: 0.0
            backbone_router_atl.bgp_advertise_summary: 0.0
            backbone_router_peer.bgp_bestpath_change: 0.4
            backbone_router_atl.participation_status_disabled: 1.0
            backbone_router_peer.neighbor_state_idle: 1.0
          latency_multipliers:
            http_req_affected_ok_f: {p50: 1.0, p95: 1.0}

              count: 1
              hosts: [atl01]

              count: 3

              count: 1
              hosts: [tm01]
          manifestation:
            [backbone_router_atl.router_disabled, backbone_router_peer.bgp_neighbor_down, edge_pop_affected.req_complete_ok, backbone_router_atl.participation_status_disabled]

        - order: 3
          at_min: 40
          component: core_observability
          event: |
            The core observability ingest pipeline develops backpressure and begins dropping some telemetry while edge request handling remains healthy.
          why: |
            Delayed ingestion pressure in the central pipeline leads to increased queue lag and explicit dropped-event warnings; this is directly observable from ingest stats and drop warnings, while customer traffic is not directly impacted in this phase.
          flows: [http_req_affected_ok_f, http_req_unaffected_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            core_observability.ingest_metric: 2.0
            core_observability.drop_warn: 1.0

          manifestation: [core_observability.ingest_metric, core_observability.drop_warn]

      steady:
        - component: core_observability
          condition: |
            Observability ingestion remains congested with elevated queue lag and intermittent dropped telemetry events.
          user_impact: |
            Customers mostly see recovered service, but operators have reduced visibility (some logs/metrics are missing or delayed), slowing verification and follow-up analysis.
          manifestation: [core_observability.drop_warn, core_observability.ingest_metric]

        - component: backbone_router_atl
          condition: |
            Atlanta remains out of backbone participation, and peers continue to show the Atlanta iBGP neighbor in a non-established state.
          user_impact: |
            Backbone paths avoid Atlanta; routing remains stable but with reduced backbone connectivity through that site.
          manifestation: [backbone_router_atl.participation_status_disabled, backbone_router_peer.neighbor_state_idle]

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_storm_overload_loop
          desc: |
            As Atlanta overload causes upstream timeouts, the edge retries internal upstream fetches toward Atlanta,
            amplifying load during the route-leak interval until Atlanta is removed from the backbone.

      flows:
        [http_req_affected_ok_f, http_req_unaffected_ok_f, http_req_affected_client_err_f, http_req_affected_to_atl_fail_f]
      manifestation:
        [edge_pop_affected.req_complete_err, edge_pop_affected.retrying_upstream, backbone_router_atl.forwarding_drops, core_observability.drop_warn]

  assumptions:
    - |
      POP identity is treated as a semantic carrier for localized impact: edge_pop_affected emits only [ewr, ord, sjc, lon, ams]

    - |
      The real incident durations (27-minute outage plus later observability disruption) are compressed to fit a short,
      analysis-friendly window while preserving the causal sequence: route leak → Atlanta overload → mitigation → telemetry drops.
    - |
      Failure-phase piecewise breakdown (effective, approximate):
      - mins 25-35 (10 min): overall entry traffic ~50% of normal (unaffected at 50%; affected error/timeout flows at 25% each);
        affected local-success suppressed; Atlanta routing/overload signals elevated.
      - mins 35-40 (5 min): customer traffic recovers near normal; Atlanta removed from backbone so overload/export-summary logs stop;
        post-mitigation neighbor/participation markers appear; residual Atlanta-routed errors/retries are rare.
      - mins 40-50 (10 min): customer traffic remains healthy; observability ingest metrics are elevated and drop warnings are present.
    - |
      One-shots per event: event1 (2), event2 (5), event3 (0).
    - |
      Visibility rationale: during mins 25-35, frequent client error completions plus retry markers (affected POPs only), along with
      router/peer routing signals and an anomaly alert, make the trigger and localized impact clear; mitigation is marked by explicit
      shutdown/neighbor-down one-shots plus ongoing disabled/idle status; the later observability issue is visible via ingest stats and
      explicit drop warnings (rather than relying on missing logs).
    - |
      Total log volume estimate: normal ~30k logs (25 min * ~1200/min) + failure ~23k-30k logs (flows dominate; moderate background) ~= ~53k-60k logs total,
      within 20k..100k.
