scenario:
  id: incident_2025_03_31_sessionstore_unavailability
  title: SessionStore outage from Cassandra disk exhaustion and quorum loss (recovered via wipe)

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    MediaWiki uses SessionStore for session reads/writes, and SessionStore relies on a Cassandra cluster with QUORUM consistency across two datacenters.
    Storage growth exhausts Cassandra disks, multiple nodes shut down, and the cluster drops below quorum.
    SessionStore begins returning HTTP 500s; sessions are lost and edit saves fail, triggering alerts and on-call investigation.
    Service is restored by wiping/truncating the session data and restarting Cassandra; availability returns quickly but all sessions are lost (disruptive recovery).

  trigger_desc: |
    Cassandra data disks reach exhaustion after sustained session-write storage growth; enough Cassandra nodes crash that QUORUM cannot be met, causing SessionStore read/write failures and HTTP 500s.

  time:
    total_minutes: 60
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 18}
      f: {start_min: 18, end_min: 60}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        MediaWiki serves page views and edit saves at steady rates.
        SessionStore reads and writes sessions successfully and Cassandra shows healthy free space and low compaction backlog.
        No alerts are active.
      flows: [mw_page_view_ok, mw_edit_save_ok, ss_cassandra_read_sample_ok, ss_cassandra_write_sample_ok]
      manifestation:
        - mediawiki_app.heartbeat
        - sessionstore_api.http_get_200
        - sessionstore_api.pool_health
        - cassandra_cluster.disk_usage

    f:
      desc: |
        Cassandra nodes begin shutting down after running out of free disk, reducing the live-node count below QUORUM.
        SessionStore cannot complete QUORUM reads/writes and returns HTTP 500 to MediaWiki.
        MediaWiki experiences session loss and widespread edit failures. Responders identify Cassandra quorum loss and disk exhaustion as the cause,
        then perform a disruptive mitigation (wipe/truncate sessions + restart) that restores availability but clears all sessions.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 18
          component: cassandra_cluster
          event: |
            Multiple Cassandra nodes hit disk exhaustion and shut down; SessionStore loses QUORUM and begins returning HTTP 500s for session reads/writes.
          why: |
            Cassandra is log-structured; sustained overwrite-heavy writes can grow on-disk data faster than compaction reclaims space.
            When disks fill, commitlog writes fail and nodes shut down, dropping the cluster below QUORUM. Shutdown/DOWN is directly observable;
            the workload change is external.
          flows:
            - mw_page_view_sessionstore_5xx
            - mw_edit_save_500
            - ss_cassandra_read_sample_fail
            - ss_cassandra_write_sample_fail
          rate_multipliers:
            mw_page_view_ok_post_wipe: 0.0
            mw_edit_save_ok_post_wipe: 0.0
            ss_cassandra_read_sample_ok_post_wipe: 0.0
            ss_cassandra_write_sample_ok_post_wipe: 0.0
            sessionstore_api.pool_health_ok: 0.0
            cassandra_cluster.disk_usage_recovered: 0.0
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_sessionstore_5xx: 0.0
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_edit_failures: 0.0
          latency_multipliers: {}

              count: 3
              hosts: [cass-a2, cass-b1, cass-b2]
            - ref: cassandra_cluster.node_marked_down
              count: 3
              hosts: [cass-a2, cass-b1, cass-b2]
          manifestation:
            - cassandra_cluster.commitlog_no_space
            - sessionstore_api.http_put_500

        - order: 2
          at_min: 23
          component: monitoring_alerts
          event: |
            Monitoring detects sustained SessionStore HTTP 5xx and elevated edit failures; paging and critical alerting begin.
          why: |
            SessionStoreErrorRateHigh is driven by sustained HTTP 500 responses from SessionStore.
            MediaWikiEditFailures is driven by session-dependent edit saves failing. Both are directly observable through alerting logs.
          flows: [mw_edit_save_500, mw_page_view_sessionstore_5xx]
          rate_multipliers:
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_sessionstore_5xx: 1.0
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_edit_failures: 1.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:

              count: 1
              hosts: [mon1]

              count: 1
              hosts: [mon1]
            - ref: monitoring_alerts.page_sent
              count: 1
              hosts: [mon1]

            - monitoring_alerts.page_sent

          at_min: 29
          component: oncall_ops
          event: |
            On-call acknowledges the page and begins investigation; SessionStore pool health logging is increased for faster feedback on quorum status.
          why: |
            Human response to paging leads to acknowledgment and additional diagnostic logging.
            The pool health signal is directly observable and corroborates loss of QUORUM across datacenters.
          flows:
            - mw_page_view_sessionstore_5xx
            - mw_edit_save_500
            - ss_cassandra_read_sample_fail
            - ss_cassandra_write_sample_fail

              hosts: [ops1]

              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
          manifestation:

            - sessionstore_api.pool_health
            - monitoring_alerts.alert_active_sessionstore_5xx

        - order: 4
          at_min: 42
          component: cassandra_cluster
          event: |
            Investigation confirms Cassandra unavailability (too many nodes down for QUORUM) and ties it to disk exhaustion/compaction pressure; one more node shuts down.
          why: |
            SessionStore pool_health (low live_hosts) indicates a cluster below QUORUM.
            Cassandra disk metrics show near-zero free space and elevated compaction backlog; shutdown/DOWN is directly observable.
          flows:
            - ss_cassandra_read_sample_fail
            - ss_cassandra_write_sample_fail

            ss_cassandra_write_sample_fail: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.6}
            mw_edit_save_500: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.5}
          one_shots:
            - ref: cassandra_cluster.commitlog_no_space
              count: 1
              hosts: [cass-b3]
            - ref: cassandra_cluster.node_marked_down
              count: 1
              hosts: [cass-b3]
            - ref: oncall_ops.investigation_note
              count: 2
              hosts: [ops1]

            - cassandra_cluster.disk_usage
            - cassandra_cluster.compaction_backlog
            - sessionstore_api.pool_health

          at_min: 52
          component: oncall_ops
          event: |
            Responders decide to wipe/truncate SessionStore Cassandra data in both datacenters to free disk and restore quorum.
          why: |
            With disks exhausted and QUORUM unattainable, truncation/wipe is a disruptive but effective way to free space and bring nodes back.
            The decision is directly observable as an operator marker; the resulting state change is confirmed by recovered metrics and reduced 5xx.
          flows: [mw_page_view_sessionstore_5xx, mw_edit_save_500]
          rate_multipliers: {}
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:
            - ref: oncall_ops.decision_log
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops1]
          manifestation:
            - oncall_ops.decision_log

        - order: 6
          at_min: 56
          component: cassandra_cluster
          event: |
            Storage is wiped/truncated and Cassandra is restarted; quorum is restored and SessionStore returns mostly-200 again, while alerts resolve.
          why: |
            Truncation frees disk space and allows Cassandra to start and participate in quorum again. This is directly observable via truncate/restart markers,
            recovered disk metrics, and SessionStore switching from sustained 5xx to mostly 200s. Session loss persists because all session rows were removed.
          flows:
            - mw_page_view_ok_post_wipe
            - mw_edit_save_ok_post_wipe
            - ss_cassandra_read_sample_ok_post_wipe

            sessionstore_api.pool_health: 0.0
            cassandra_cluster.disk_usage_recovered: 1.0
            cassandra_cluster.disk_usage: 0.0
            cassandra_cluster.compaction_backlog: 0.1
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_sessionstore_5xx: 0.0
            monitoring_alerts.alert_active_edit_failures: 0.0
          latency_multipliers: {}
          one_shots:

              count: 2

              count: 1
              hosts: [mon1]
            - ref: monitoring_alerts.alert_resolved_edit_failures
              count: 1
              hosts: [mon1]
          manifestation:
            - cassandra_cluster.truncate_sessions
            - cassandra_cluster.cassandra_restart
            - sessionstore_api.http_put_200
            - monitoring_alerts.alert_resolved_sessionstore_5xx

      steady:
        - component: sessionstore_api
          condition: |
            After wipe/restart, SessionStore is available again and serves mostly HTTP 200; a small residual 5xx rate may persist briefly during stabilization.
          user_impact: |
            Users are forced to re-establish sessions because all session data was wiped; edit throughput returns, but some users see session loss/logouts.
          manifestation:
            - sessionstore_api.http_get_200
            - sessionstore_api.http_put_200

      feedback_loops:
        - id: retry_pressure_on_remaining_nodes
          desc: |
            As nodes go down, the remaining nodes face higher relative pressure and longer tail latencies.
            SessionStore retry behavior (visible in sampled internal telemetry) can increase load and prolong unavailability until disk is freed.

      flows:
        - mw_page_view_sessionstore_5xx
        - mw_edit_save_500
        - ss_cassandra_read_sample_fail
        - ss_cassandra_write_sample_fail
        - mw_page_view_ok_post_wipe
        - mw_edit_save_ok_post_wipe
        - ss_cassandra_read_sample_ok_post_wipe
        - ss_cassandra_write_sample_ok_post_wipe
      manifestation:

    - |
      Failure-phase piecewise breakdown (scenario minutes):
      - 18-23 (5m): outage begins; alerts suppressed; Cassandra shutdown/DOWN one-shots occur.
      - 23-56 (33m): alerts active; investigation increases pool health logging; compaction backlog elevated; edits mostly failing.
      - 56-60 (4m): wipe/truncate + restart; service restored; alerts resolve; sessions wiped (residual user disruption).
    - |

    - |

      - Failure outage (38m of 42m): ~23k flow logs (incl. sampled retries) + ~400 background logs + one-shots.
      - Post-wipe (4m): ~2k flow logs + ~40 background logs + one-shots (degraded pool_health/disk_usage suppressed; OK/recovered variants enabled).
      Total ~35k logs, within 20k–100k.
    - |
      Visibility rationale: outage onset is evident from sustained SessionStore HTTP 500s plus degraded sessionstore_api.pool_health and Cassandra shutdown/DOWN one-shots,
      corroborated by Cassandra disk_usage near 0–5% and increased compaction_backlog. Recovery is evident from truncate/restart one-shots, disk_usage_recovered,
      SessionStore switching to mostly-200, and alert_resolved markers; contradictory degraded disk_usage/pool_health emissions are suppressed after the wipe.
