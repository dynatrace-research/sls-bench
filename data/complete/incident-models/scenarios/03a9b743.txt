```yaml
scenario:
  id: dynamodb_us_east_membership_retry_storm
  title: Metadata membership timeout causes storage node self-disqualification and retry storm

  states: {n: normal, f: failure}

  summary: |
    DynamoDB storage nodes periodically renew their partition membership from an internal metadata service.
    A brief network disruption synchronized membership renewals, and some membership responses were slow due to
    significantly larger membership payloads (driven by rapid GSI adoption) and insufficient metadata-service capacity.
    Storage nodes timed out, disqualified themselves from serving traffic, and retried membership fetches, sustaining
    overload and driving elevated customer API error rates.

  trigger_desc: |
    A brief network disruption affects a subset of storage nodes, causing many nodes to simultaneously request
    membership renewals from the metadata service.

  time:
    total_minutes: 30
    phases:
      n: {start_min: 0, end_min: 15}
      f: {start_min: 15, end_min: 30}

  phases:
    n:
      desc: |
        Customer read/write requests are served by eligible storage nodes at low latency.
        Storage nodes renew membership periodically; metadata service queue depth is low and membership build times
        remain well within the storage deadline.
      flows: [customer_rw, membership_renewal]
      manifestation:
        - api_frontend.resp_ok
        - metadata_service.queue_metrics
        - metadata_service.membership_resp
        - storage_node.membership_applied

    f:
      desc: |
        A synchronized membership-renewal surge hits the metadata service. Membership payload sizes are larger than
        historical norms, so concurrent requests push build times beyond the storage-node deadline. As timeouts occur,
        storage nodes self-disqualify and customer requests fail when routed to ineligible nodes. Retries keep pressure
        high on the metadata service, including failed administrative scaling attempts, until operators pause membership
        fetches to reduce the retry storm and regain control of the metadata service.

      events:
        - order: 1
          at_min: 15
          component: storage_node
          event: |
            A brief network disruption causes multiple storage nodes to refresh membership at the same time, increasing
            concurrent membership fetches and pushing metadata service load upward.
          why: |
            The disruption is directly visible via storage-node network markers; the membership surge is visible as a jump
            in membership fetch attempts and metadata-service load metrics. Larger membership payloads (from GSI-driven
            partition growth) make some builds close to the storage deadline, increasing timeout likelihood.
          flows: [membership_renewal_timeout_f, membership_renewal_success_f, customer_rw_err_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: 1.4
            metadata_service.queue_metrics: 1.3
          latency_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: {p50: 1.2, p95: 1.4}
          one_shots:
            - ref: storage_node.net_disruption
              count: 2
              hosts: [stor-01, stor-02]
          manifestation:
            - storage_node.net_disruption
            - metadata_service.queue_metrics
            - metadata_service.slow_build
            - storage_node.membership_timeout

        - order: 2
          at_min: 21
          component: metadata_service
          event: |
            Membership timeouts and retries amplify, creating sustained overload; more storage nodes become disqualified,
            and customer API errors rise toward a majority of requests.
          why: |
            Retries from disqualified nodes keep inflight work and queue depth high (visible in queue_metrics), and more
            slow_membership_build logs appear due to large payload sizes and reduced effective capacity. Admin scaling
            attempts fail because overload prevents administrative requests from completing.
          flows: [membership_renewal_timeout_f, customer_rw_err_f, admin_scale_attempt_fail_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: 2.0
            membership_renewal_success_f: 1.2
            customer_rw_err_f: 1.2
            customer_rw_ok_f: 0.8
            admin_scale_attempt_fail_f: 2.0
            metadata_service.queue_metrics: 2.0
          latency_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: {p50: 1.5, p95: 2.0}
            membership_renewal_success_f: {p50: 1.3, p95: 1.8}
          one_shots: []
          manifestation:
            - metadata_service.queue_metrics
            - metadata_service.slow_build
            - storage_node.membership_retry
            - api_frontend.resp_err
            - metadata_service.admin_api_err

        - order: 3
          at_min: 26
          component: ops_control
          event: |
            Operators pause storage-node membership fetches to stop the retry storm, then successfully add metadata-service
            capacity once administrative requests can complete. Metadata load eases, but many storage nodes remain out of
            the request path so customer errors stay elevated.
          why: |
            Pausing membership fetches removes the primary source of retries (directly observable via the operator action log
            and a sharp reduction in membership renewal attempts). With load relieved, the metadata service can accept
            administrative scaling (observable as an admin_api status=200 marker). Requalification is not immediate because
            membership fetches are paused and nodes remain disqualified.
          flows: [customer_rw_err_f, customer_rw_ok_f]
          rate_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: 0.0
            membership_renewal_success_f: 0.0
            admin_scale_attempt_fail_f: 0.0
            customer_rw_err_f: 0.6
            customer_rw_ok_f: 1.0
            metadata_service.queue_metrics: 0.5
          latency_multipliers:
            membership_renewal_timeout_f: {p50: 0.9, p95: 1.1}
            membership_renewal_success_f: {p50: 0.9, p95: 1.1}
          one_shots:
            - ref: ops_control.pause_membership_requests
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-01]
            - ref: metadata_service.admin_api_ok
              count: 1
              hosts: [meta-01]
            - ref: ops_control.scale_capacity_applied
              count: 1
              hosts: [ops-01]
          manifestation:
            - ops_control.pause_membership_requests
            - metadata_service.admin_api_ok
            - metadata_service.queue_metrics
            - api_frontend.resp_err

      steady:
        - component: storage_node
          condition: |
            Membership fetches remain paused, leaving a significant subset of storage nodes disqualified/eligible=false and
            unable to rejoin the customer request path by the end of the scenario.
          user_impact: |
            Customer API requests intermittently fail (e.g., 503/504) when routed to disqualified nodes; overall capacity is
            reduced, so some successful operations may be slower than normal.
          manifestation:
            - storage_node.heartbeat
            - storage_node.serve_reject
            - api_frontend.resp_err

        - component: metadata_service
          condition: |
            Retry pressure is relieved after the pause, and the service can process administrative scaling; load indicators
            are reduced compared to the peak but the system remains in an incident posture while storage nodes are out.
          user_impact: |
            Control-plane actions can proceed, but customer-facing availability remains degraded due to storage nodes still
            being disqualified and not renewing membership.
          manifestation:
            - metadata_service.queue_metrics
            - metadata_kvstore.latency_metrics

      feedback_loops:
        - id: membership_retry_storm
          desc: |
            Membership fetch timeouts cause storage nodes to retry; retries increase metadata service concurrency and queue depth,
            which further increases build time and produces more timeouts. Disqualification reduces serving capacity, increasing
            customer errors without directly reducing membership retry pressure until traffic is explicitly paused.

      flows:
        - customer_rw_ok_f
        - customer_rw_err_f
        - membership_renewal_success_f
        - membership_renewal_timeout_f
        - admin_scale_attempt_fail_f
      manifestation:
        - metadata_service.queue_metrics
        - metadata_service.slow_build
        - storage_node.membership_timeout
        - storage_node.membership_retry
        - api_frontend.resp_err
        - ops_control.pause_membership_requests

  assumptions:
    - >
      Timeline is compressed: minutes 15..30 represent the early incident progression (network disruption → retry storm → pause+scale),
      without modeling full restoration; the scenario ends while still in a degraded failure state as required.
    - >
      Failure-phase piecewise volume (rough): 15..21 (6 min) elevated membership timeouts (~1.4x) ≈ 2.7k logs/min;
      21..26 (5 min) peak retry storm (~2.0x) ≈ 3.3k logs/min; 26..30 (4 min) post-pause with membership renewals stopped
      (0.0x) ≈ 0.85k logs/min, with three one-shot operator/admin markers at minute 26.
    - >
      Normal-phase volume (15 min) ≈ 1.4k logs/min (~21k logs total). Total scenario volume ≈ 55k–60k logs, within 20k..100k.
    - >
      Key diagnostic visibility: slow_membership_build (bytes/elapsed_ms/partitions), membership_fetch_timeout + retries,
      and metadata queue_metrics are high-rate during the early/mid failure, while the pause is unambiguous via ops_control.pause_membership_requests
      plus a sudden disappearance of membership renewal flows.
```
