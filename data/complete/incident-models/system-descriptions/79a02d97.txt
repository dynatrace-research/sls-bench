assumptions:
  - |-
    Repair: changed flows.f.health_check_eqiad_ok.rpm from 0.2 to 6.0, and rely on scenario multipliers to keep it at 0.0 from f.start_min
    until recovery; this prevents early probe_ok logs while still allowing probes to shift from FAIL->OK later without inflating total probe volume.
  - |-
    Names are genericized; "OpenSearch cluster-manager" corresponds to the leader/master role mentioned in the post-mortem.
  - |-
    Node cirrussearch1100 is modeled as a separate component (opensearch_eqiad_node1100) so that after a stop action its failure-state
    background emissions can be driven to 0 and later restored on start.
  - |-
    Router periodic pooling-state snapshots are modeled as two distinct background logs (pooled vs depooled) and scenario multipliers
    ensure only one is active at a time, preventing contradictory snapshots during depool/repool transitions.
  - |-
    To avoid modeling the same user request lifecycle twice with an uncorrelatable req_id, the gateway emits two distinct flow families in failure:
    (a) client-facing search requests (search_query_eqiad_client_503) identified by req_id, and
    (b) internal upstream attempt activity (search_query_eqiad_fail) identified by upstream_id and retry markers.
    These IDs are intentionally not correlatable in this simplified model.
  - |-
    Recovery and repooling are modeled within the failure phase by adding a failure-state success flow (search_query_eqiad_ok_recovered)
    that is suppressed early and enabled only after the repool event; this keeps the required two-phase structure while capturing the incident resolution.
  - |-
    The split-brain mechanism is represented by EQIAD core-node leader warnings (opensearch_eqiad.no_cluster_manager),
    the bad node's join mismatch warnings (opensearch_eqiad_node1100.cluster_state_mismatch), and gateway-observed upstream 503s plus monitoring probe failures.
  - |-
    OpenSearch per-query logs are not modeled; user-visible failures are represented by gateway client-facing 503 logs and monitoring probes/alerts.
  - |-
    Cluster health snapshots always report a concrete master node name (never "none"); leaderless conditions are modeled via explicit
    no_cluster_manager warnings and gateway/probe 503s, preventing accidental leaderless signals during healthy/recovered moments.
  - |-
    Traffic volume is scaled to keep total logs in the 20k..100k target range while keeping key signals frequent enough to be visible.
  - |-
    Estimated total volume at modeled rates over the scenario is ~30k logs (dominant contributor is per-request gateway logs, plus internal upstream-attempt logs during the failure onset).
