    the primary endpoint. During an RDS Multi-AZ failover, the API briefly cannot connect to the DB
    and returns elevated 5xx responses. With a non-ACID durability setting (innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=2),
    a small window of committed transactions can be missing on the new primary after failover, later causing
    replica SQL thread crashes with duplicate-key errors.

assumptions:
  - >-
    Names/hosts are genericized; "RDS control plane" represents AWS RDS event and snapshot progress visibility.
  - >-
    The five replicas are modeled as one pool component emitting per-replica status logs on five hosts; the
    duplicate-key "SQL thread stopped" error is modeled as a discrete stop-point (scenario one-shots), not a continuous stream.
  - >-
    The causal core preserved: Multi-AZ failover causes brief DB connectivity loss; with
    innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=2 and sync_binlog=1, a small window of transactions can exist in
    binlogs/traces but not on the new primary, later causing duplicate-key replication crashes.
  - >-
    Snapshot/replica rebuild is represented by snapshot_start (one-shot), snapshot_progress (periodic), and an
    AWS support note; the real 6-hour completion is compressed and not modeled as completing within the scenario.
  - >-
    Normal log volume estimate is ~1000 logs/min (reads 360 rpm *2 + writes 90 rpm *3 + light background), over
    15 minutes ≈ 15k logs.
  - >-
    Total scenario log volume estimate is ~60k–80k logs depending on multipliers and background, within 20k–100k.
  - >-
    Visibility anchors: api_service.http_access_5xx and db_connect_error during failover; rds_controlplane
    failover markers (one-shots); rds_replicas.replication_status showing SQL stopped/lag after failover plus a one-time
    replication_error stop-point; snapshot_progress once rebuild starts; and ops variable check/set showing innodb_flush 2→1.
