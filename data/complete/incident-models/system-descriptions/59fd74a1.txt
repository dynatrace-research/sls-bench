    The modeled incident is driven by janitor authentication breakage after a password rotation, leaking VMs until vSphere

assumptions:
  - |
    Names/infra are simplified: MacStadium/EMC/Xserve/vSphere are represented as a single vSphere cluster component plus three services (scheduler, VM manager, janitor) and a metrics/alerting component.
  - |
    The real leak accumulated over multiple days; the model compresses this into a short window where the consequences become visible (high VM inventory and admission denials).
  - |
    Normal-state rates are set so VM creation and deletion can balance while keeping inventory in the low-hundreds: ~20 successful VM starts/min with ~20 VM deletions/min.
    This roughly corresponds to ~200 concurrent VMs with ~10 minute average build lifetime (not modeled explicitly).
  - |
    Janitor metric defect is modeled by switching between vm_total_report_stale (150..220) and vm_total_report_live (0..7000), with corresponding metrics ingestion logs; alert evaluation remains status=ok throughout.
  - |
    Normal-phase volume estimate: ~20 rpm * 4 logs ≈ 80 logs/min plus ~29 logs/min background ≈ 110 logs/min.
  - |
    Scenario total volume estimate (with leak-failure traffic and a high-rate aggressive cleanup burst) is ~21k logs, within the 20k..100k target, while keeping key diagnostic signals (auth failures, stale metrics, inventory-high snapshots, admission denials, re-queues, pauses) likely to appear.
