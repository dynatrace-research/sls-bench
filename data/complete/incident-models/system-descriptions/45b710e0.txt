    A packet-switched network of IMP nodes provides connectivity between TIP terminal servers and remote hosts.
    IMPs exchange routing updates using a flooding protocol with sequence numbers to keep the network-wide view consistent.
    During the incident, corrupted routing-update sequence numbers form a cycle that is repeatedly accepted and forwarded,
    driving high CPU and buffer starvation in IMPs and causing neighbor lines to be declared down, leading to widespread "net trouble" for users.

assumptions:
  - |-
    System is modeled as TIPs, multiple IMP nodes, and an NCC; host systems beyond TIP/IMP are abstracted into dst_host fields.
  - |-
    The causal core is preserved: corrupted routing update sequence numbers for origin IMP50 form a cycle, causing indefinite flooding/processing.
  - |-
    The hardware malfunction and bit-dropping in/near IMP29 is not directly logged; it is inferred from the repeating seq set {8,40,44} and origin=imp50.
  - |-
    Line up/down protocol details (HELLO/I-HEARD-YOU) are represented via line_state reason=hello_timeout and buffer_starvation symptom=hello_drop.
  - |-
    Normal-phase volume estimate (15 min): ~850 logs/min from flows + ~13 logs/min background across 6 IMPs => ~12k-13k logs.
  - |-
    With failure routing storm modeled at thousands of logs/min, total scenario volume is expected ~60k logs (target 20k-100k), making key signals (ru_rx_cycle, cpu_warn, buffer_low, line_state, tip net_trouble) very likely to appear.
