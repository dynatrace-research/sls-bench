sys:
  id: tq_cluster_startup_logging_stall
  desc: >
    A large MMO game cluster runs on many worker nodes coordinated by a master orchestrator ("Polaris").
    During maintenance and deployments, all nodes must advance through startup stages (-4..0) before the cluster opens to players.
    Nodes load sovereignty campaign data during stage -1 and emit developer-oriented logs to a central log collector.
    In this incident model, a specific "campaign" logging channel becomes backpressured, causing nodes to stall and miss heartbeats so the cluster cannot complete startup.

states: {n: normal, f: failure}

assumptions:
  - |
      The real cluster has ~250 nodes; this model uses 12 representative worker hosts while keeping node IDs in logs in the 1..250 range
      to preserve observability of "many nodes stuck/dead" without simulating 250 separate hosts.
  - |
      The causal core is modeled as synchronous/backpressured logging on a specific "campaign" channel causing node stalls and missed
      heartbeats, preventing Polaris from advancing startup past stage -1.
  - |
      Campaign data volume and exact sovereignty mechanics are abstracted into campaign_log_line flow traffic plus logger_backpressure
      and log_collector.queue_high corroboration.
  - |
      Normal-phase volume estimate: ~250 rpm * 2 logs/req ≈ 500 logs/min from logins, plus ~75 logs/min from background across nodes
      and infrastructure.
      Total estimated logs for the scenario: ~30k–45k, within the 20k–100k target.
  - |
      log_collector.ingest_stats.eps is modeled to match this spec's overall emission envelope (order ~10–30 eps, with headroom),
      rather than real-world Splunk/cluster-scale event rates.
