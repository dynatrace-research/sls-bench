    A managed RavenDB Cloud fleet runs secured RavenDB clusters where nodes and external clients authenticate using TLS/X509.
    Nodes maintain long-lived pooled connections, so new TLS handshakes are relatively infrequent under normal load.
    The incident models a partial loss of trust for a Let’s Encrypt chain after an OS trust-store update, causing new node-to-node
    and occasional client connections to fail TLS validation while existing connections keep working.

assumptions:
  - The fleet is modeled as 6 RavenDB nodes to represent many isolated customer clusters across regions/providers; connectivity issues affect a subset but are modeled via flow rates rather than per-cluster topology.
  - The core mechanism is an OS trust-store change (ca-certificates update removing DST Root CA X3) combined with serving/using an older Let’s Encrypt chain (represented as chain=dst_root_ca_x3), causing new TLS handshakes to fail while established pooled connections often continue.
  - Cluster splits primarily impact cluster-wide/quorum tasks (modeled as cluster_task_deploy_index_fail_f) while regular reads/writes remain mostly available (client_api_request_f continues with high RPM and success status codes).
  - Normal-state cluster_connect_n allows up to 3 attempts to align with the shared retry log template (cluster_connect_retry) used in both normal and failure flows; retries remain rare via expected_attempts≈1.07.
  - Inbound client handshake failures are modeled as server-side observations (TLS alert/EOF) rather than client-side chain-validation details; explicit chain-validation evidence comes from ravendb_node.cluster_peer_untrusted and monitoring.probe_tls_fail.
  - Normal-phase expected logs/min ≈ 900 (client) + small background/probe/connect/task noise; scenario-sized to yield ~29k–35k total logs (target 20k–100k) while keeping rare client-facing handshake failures visible but low-rate.
