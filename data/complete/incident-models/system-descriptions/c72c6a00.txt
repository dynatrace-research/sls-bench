assumptions:
  - >
    The model focuses on external reachability and BGP announcement coverage; customer VM-side logs are not modeled because
    inbound traffic loss produces little/no VM-local telemetry.
  - >
    The edge fabric is represented by a single control-plane emitter for global BGP coverage summaries plus per-site apply
    logs (site is a field), rather than modeling dozens of edge routers explicitly.
  - >
    The netcfg push flows include a return hop to netcfg_mgr (push completion), so edge_bgp_fabric includes a modeled telemetry/ack edge
    back to netcfg_mgr for path connectivity.
  - >
    The rollout controller does not explicitly log that it is ignoring a failing canary; instead, the bug is inferred from the
    close timing of netcfg_mgr.canary_failed followed by netcfg_mgr.rollout_continues and subsequent edge withdrawals.
  - >
    The real incident included an earlier asia-east1 VPN failure and a long investigation window; this model compresses those precursors
    but preserves the key beats: bad rendered config, canary failure not stopping rollout, progressive withdrawal, full inbound loss,
    and restoration by rollback while incident response continues (change freeze/monitoring).
  - >
    Added a low-rate failure-state background log (sre_ops.change_freeze_status) to make the "freeze remains enabled" end-state observable
    beyond the one-shot freeze-enablement marker.
  - >
    Volume estimate (60 minutes): normal ~16k request logs + low background; failure ~33k-36k request+retry logs + alerts and
    coverage metrics; total ~49k-53k logs, within the 20k-100k target and with high-signal anchors (coverage zero, probe timeouts,
    inbound-loss alerts, rollback markers, restored coverage).
